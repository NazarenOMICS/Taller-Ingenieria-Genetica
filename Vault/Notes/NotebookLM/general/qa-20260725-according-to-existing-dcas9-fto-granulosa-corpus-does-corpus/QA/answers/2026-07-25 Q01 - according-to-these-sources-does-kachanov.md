# Q01 - According to these sources, does Kachanov et al. (2025) provide any direct evidence about why FTO is basally low or repressed (for example promoter state, H3K27ac, chromatin repression, methylation, transcription factor loss), or does it only show technical activation of endogenous FTO with dCas9-p300? Also, which concrete results from that paper most strongly support that activating FTO with dCas9-p300 could plausibly work in our granulosa project, and what key caveats prevent overclaiming transferability?

Based on the provided sources, a paper by **Kachanov et al. (2025)** is **not included in the current notebook**. The sources from 2025 are authored by **Li et al. (2025)**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] and **Zhang et al. (2025)**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]].

If you are referring to **Zhang et al. (2025)**, this paper does **not** show technical activation of endogenous **FTO** with **dCas9-p300**. In fact, they explicitly used *Fto* as part of a **"random gene set"** for control purposes, demonstrating that its expression remained **unchanged** during follicular maturation and was unaffected by their targeted perturbation of the *Fosl2* pathway in granulosa cells (GCs)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 2|[3]]]. 

However, other sources within your collection provide relevant evidence regarding FTO regulation and the feasibility of using dCas9-p300 for its activation in your granulosa project:

### Direct Evidence of FTO Repression
While the sources do not mention Kachanov (2025), **Zewei et al. (2024)** and **Li et al. (2025)** offer insights into why FTO might be basally low or repressed:
*   **Transcriptional Repression and Chromatin State:** FTO expression is negatively regulated by the transcription factor **FOXA2**, which binds to the *FTO* promoter  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 3|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 4|[5]]]. This repression is mediated by the **histone deacetylase HDAC3**, suggesting a role for **chromatin repression** via deacetylation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 3|[4]]].
*   **Signaling Inhibition:** The **Wnt/β-catenin** pathway has been shown to inhibit *FTO* expression by recruiting β-catenin/TCF/LEF to elements in its promoter  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 5|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/li_2025_mechanism_of_crosstalk_between_dna_methylation_and_histone.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]].
*   **Self-Regulatory Repression:** FTO can act as its own transcriptional repressor by binding to its promoter; this binding is hindered by **Fe²⁺**, which leads to increased FTO levels  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/xinru_2024_the_role_of_epigenetics_in_women_s_reproductive.pdf#Passage 1|[8]]].
*   **Disease-Related Downregulation:** In **premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)** and **senescent granulosa cells**, *FTO* mRNA and protein levels are **significantly decreased**, leading to increased m6A levels and faster senescence  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/daan_2017_dcas9_a_versatile_tool_for_epigenome_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/zheng_2026_histone_modifications_in_cardiovascular_disease_mechanisms_and_therapeutic.pdf#Passage 1|[10]]].

### Support for dCas9-p300 Activation in GCs
Concrete results from the sources suggest that activating FTO with **dCas9-p300** is a plausible strategy for your project:
*   **Locus-Selective Activation:** dCas9-p300 is a established "epigenome editor" that directly catalyzes **H3K27ac** at targeted promoters and distal enhancers, successfully activating endogenous gene transcription  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/matthew_2019_sufficiency_analysis_of_estrogen_responsive_enhancers_using_synthetic.pdf#Passage 1|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/matthew_2019_sufficiency_analysis_of_estrogen_responsive_enhancers_using_synthetic.pdf#Passage 2|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/matthew_2019_sufficiency_analysis_of_estrogen_responsive_enhancers_using_synthetic.pdf#Passage 3|[13]]]. 
*   **Successful Distal Activation:** Results from **Matthew et al. (2019)** demonstrate that dCas9-p300 can activate genes from distal enhancers—often resulting in equal or greater activation than targeting the promoter directly—in a manner that mimics physiological induction  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/matthew_2019_sufficiency_analysis_of_estrogen_responsive_enhancers_using_synthetic.pdf#Passage 4|[14]]].
*   **Therapeutic Potential in GCs:** Because FTO is a "senescence-delaying protein" in granulosa cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/daan_2017_dcas9_a_versatile_tool_for_epigenome_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]], using dCas9-p300 to rescue its expression could theoretically retard ovarian aging  [[Notes/NotebookLM/general/qa-20260725-according-to-existing-dcas9-fto-granulosa-corpus-does-corpus/Sources/daan_2017_dcas9_a_versatile_tool_for_epigenome_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]].

### Key Caveats and Constraints
To prevent overclaiming transferability, several caveats highlighted in the sources should be considered:
*   **Acetylation is Not Always Sufficient:** Induction of H3K27ac at a regulatory element is **not fully predictive** of target gene activation [16]. Some loci may require the recruitment of additional cofactors (like those recruited by VP16) to assemble the transcriptional machinery [17].
*   **Context-Dependent Efficiency:** The strength of activation by dCas9-p300 is best predicted by the **baseline level of RNA Polymerase II** already present at the site [18].
*   **Locus Constraints:** Some genes are less effectively activated by CRISPRa due to **locus-dependent chromatin constraints** [19]. 
*   **Delivery and Safety:** In vivo clinical translation faces hurdles regarding **tissue-restricted delivery** and potential **guide-independent, genome-wide shifts** in H3K27ac if editor exposure is not tightly controlled [19, 20].
