# Q02 - Did any of those studies examine chromatin-level mechanisms, promoter occupancy, histone marks, or regulatory DNA changes to explain FTO downregulation?

Several of the provided sources examine or propose chromatin-level and regulatory DNA mechanisms to explain changes in FTO expression, particularly through **DNA methylation**, **transcription factor activity**, and **chromatin remodeling** driven by metabolic stress.

### 1. DNA Methylation and Promoter Changes
The sources identify DNA methylation as a primary mechanism regulating the *FTO* gene across various metabolic contexts:
*   **Obesity and Adipose Tissue:** Evidence indicates that **promoter hypermethylation** silences the *FTO* gene, which is associated with a reduced risk of obesity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]]. Conversely, hypomethylation of the *FTO* promoter—often induced by inactivity or a high-fat diet—markedly upregulates its expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]].
*   **Type 2 Diabetes (Pancreatic Islets):** A genome-wide DNA methylation analysis of human pancreatic islets from patients with type 2 diabetes identified *FTO* as one of the genes showing **significant changes in DNA methylation levels**, suggesting this epigenetic modification contributes to the disease-associated alterations in its expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yanlin_2023_epigenetic_regulation_in_metabolic_diseases_mechanisms_and_advances.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]].

### 2. Transcription Factor Occupancy
In cardiac tissue, the regulation of *FTO* transcription is linked to specific DNA-binding proteins:
*   **Cardiomyocytes (SP1 Activity):** The transcription factor **SP1** has been identified as a positive regulator that directly activates the transcription of *FTO* in cardiomyocytes  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 5|[4]]]. This mechanism is part of a pathway that maintains mitochondrial integrity and limits oxidative stress; thus, changes in SP1 activity or its occupancy at the *FTO* promoter would directly impact *FTO* expression levels  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 5|[4]]].

### 3. Chromatin Remodeling and Metabolic Signaling
The sources discuss how metabolic stress and oncometabolites influence the epigenetic landscape surrounding the *FTO* locus:
*   **Sepsis-Induced Myocardial Dysfunction (Histone Lactylation):** While direct evidence is still emerging, the sources propose a conceptual model where **histone lactylation-mediated chromatin remodeling** alters the transcriptional accessibility of the *FTO* locus  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 7|[5]]]. Under septic conditions, profound metabolic rewiring leads to lactate accumulation, which may serve as an epigenetic donor to modify chromatin "openness" at the *FTO* gene  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 7|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 8|[6]]].
*   **Cardiomyopathy (R-2HG):** In the context of dilated cardiomyopathy, the metabolite **R-2-hydroxyglutaric acid (R-2HG)**, produced by mutant IDH enzymes, has been reported to **reduce the expression of the *FTO* gene**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/wang_2026_methylation_centric_epigenetic_regulation_in_dilated_cardiomyopathy_mechanisms.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]]. This reduction in expression follows an initial phase where R-2HG directly inhibits FTO’s enzymatic activity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/wang_2026_methylation_centric_epigenetic_regulation_in_dilated_cardiomyopathy_mechanisms.pdf#Passage 2|[8]]].

### 4. Other Regulatory Mechanisms
While the query focuses on chromatin, it is worth noting that some tissues regulate FTO levels post-translationally through the **ubiquitin system**:
*   **Cerebral Ischemia-Reperfusion:** In the brain, **USP18** has been shown to stabilize the FTO protein by preventing its degradation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/zhang_2026_ubiquitination_dependent_regulation_of_ferroptosis_in_ischemic_heart.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]]. While this is not a chromatin-level change to the DNA itself, it represents a critical regulatory mechanism for maintaining FTO levels under stress in non-granulosa tissue  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/zhang_2026_ubiquitination_dependent_regulation_of_ferroptosis_in_ischemic_heart.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]].

> Ferritinophagy-dependent iron release is another major source of ubiquitin-controlled ferroptotic iron in the reperfused brain. Li et al. have reported that USP14 directly deubiquitinates and stabilizes NCOA4, which enhances ferritinophagy and promotes the release of ferritin bound iron into the labile iron pool [33]. Through this pathway, ferritin stores are converted into redox active iron and neuronal fer- roptosis is amplified during cerebral I/R. In contrast, Zhang et al. have identified a protective ubiquitin-dependent pathway that acts on the same ferritinophagy machinery at an upstream level [84]. They have shown that USP18 stabilizes fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO), and maintained FTO activity suppresses NCOA4 expression after transcription. As a result, ferritinophagy-dependent iron release is reduced, ferritin-derived iron mobilization is limited, and neuronal ferroptosis is attenuated in cerebral I/R. Thus, both USP14 and USP18 affect NCOA4 dependent ferritinophagy, but they act in opposite directions. USP14 promotes ferroptosis by directly stabilizing NCOA4, whereas USP18 suppresses ferroptosis by reducing NCOA4 expression through FTO. These studies indicate that the ubiquitin system is not simply associated with iron overload during reperfusion. It directly controls how iron is taken up, retained, buffered, and released, and in this way influences ferroptosis in the ischemic heart and brain.
