# Q05 - Which non-granulosa mechanisms are the most plausible to transfer into the granulosa oxidative-stress context, and which remain too speculative?

Based on the evidence from non-granulosa stress models, the following mechanisms are the most plausible to transfer into the granulosa oxidative-stress context, while others remain speculative due to lack of direct evidence or high tissue specificity.

### **Most Plausible Mechanisms for Transfer**

*   **FTO–PGC-1α Axis (Mitochondrial Biogenesis):** In cardiomyocytes, skeletal muscle, and renal cells, FTO directly stabilizes *PGC-1α* mRNA by removing m6A marks, thereby promoting mitochondrial biogenesis and energy metabolism  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 11|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 5|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 11|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 12|[4]]]. Given that **mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) depletion and bioenergetic failure** are central to premature ovarian insufficiency (POI) and granulosa cell aging, this axis is a highly probable candidate for explaining how FTO downregulation exacerbates oxidative damage in the ovary  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 2|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 3|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 4|[8]]].
*   **SP1-Mediated Transcriptional Activation:** The transcription factor **SP1** has been identified as a direct positive regulator of *FTO* transcription in both heart and kidney stress models  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 5|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 6|[9]]]. Because SP1 is a ubiquitous regulator and FTO is known to be downregulated in aged or stressed granulosa cells, a reduction in SP1 activity or its promoter occupancy is a plausible explanation for the observed loss of FTO expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 5|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 4|[8]]].
*   **FTO–BNIP3/Mitophagy Pathway:** In cardiac sepsis models, FTO stabilizes *BNIP3* transcripts to trigger **mitophagic clearance** of damaged mitochondria, which limits the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 11|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 12|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 13|[11]]]. Since defective autophagy and the accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria are hallmark features of granulosa cell senescence, the failure of this FTO-dependent quality control mechanism is highly likely to occur in the ovary  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 5|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 6|[13]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 7|[14]]].
*   **Promoter Hypermethylation:** In metabolic tissues like adipose and pancreatic islets, **DNA hypermethylation of the *FTO* promoter** is a primary mechanism for gene silencing  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[15]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[16]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 3|[17]]]. As the granulosa microenvironment undergoes profound epigenetic remodeling during oxidative stress, the recruitment of DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) to the *FTO* locus is a plausible chromatin-level repressive mechanism  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/rosngela_2016_oxidative_stress_and_disease.pdf#Passage 1|[18]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 8|[19]]].
*   **FTO–FOXO Axis:** FTO is known to participate in a regulatory axis with **FOXO1** to manage metabolic health  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/emina_2020_the_role_of_oxidative_stress_in_the_development.pdf#Passage 1|[20]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/mengwei_2022_trends_in_insulin_resistance_insights_into_mechanisms_and.pdf#Passage 1|[21]]]. FOXO transcription factors are critical determinants of oocyte and granulosa cell survival under oxidative stress, suggesting this crosstalk may be conserved in the reproductive system  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 9|[22]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 8|[23]]].

### **Mechanisms That Remain Speculative**

*   **Histone Lactylation-Mediated Remodeling:** While metabolic rewiring in the septic heart suggests that **histone lactylation** might alter the chromatin accessibility of the *FTO* locus, the sources explicitly state that **"direct evidence... is currently lacking"** even in cardiac models  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 7|[24]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 13|[25]]]. Its application to the granulosa context remains a purely conceptual model  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/wang_2026_methylation_centric_epigenetic_regulation_in_dilated_cardiomyopathy_mechanisms.pdf#Passage 1|[26]]].
*   **R-2HG–CEBPA Positive Feedback Loop:** In leukemia and some cardiomyopathies, the metabolite R-2HG represses *FTO* transcription by suppressing **CEBPA**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 10|[27]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 11|[28]]]. While oxidative stress in granulosa cells involves metabolic shifts, there is no direct evidence in the sources that granulosa cells produce sufficient levels of FTO-inhibiting metabolites like R-2HG to trigger this specific feedback loop  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/zhang_2026_ubiquitination_dependent_regulation_of_ferroptosis_in_ischemic_heart.pdf#Passage 1|[29]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/zhang_2026_ubiquitination_dependent_regulation_of_ferroptosis_in_ischemic_heart.pdf#Passage 2|[30]]].
*   **USP18-Mediated Protein Stabilization:** In brain tissue, **USP18** has been shown to stabilize the FTO protein  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 4|[31]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/wang_2026_methylation_centric_epigenetic_regulation_in_dilated_cardiomyopathy_mechanisms.pdf#Passage 5|[32]]]. While post-translational stabilization is a critical regulatory layer, the provided sources do not document the presence or activity of the USP18–FTO axis in granulosa cells, making its transfer speculative without further validation.
*   **Lamin-Associated Domain (LAD) Reorganization:** In dilated cardiomyopathy, changes in DNA methylation are linked to the reorganization of **lamin-associated domains**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 12|[33]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 13|[34]]]. While POI involves nuclear structural changes, the specific relocation of the *FTO* gene relative to the nuclear lamina in response to oxidative stress has not been investigated in granulosa cells [35, 36].

> Ferritinophagy-dependent iron release is another major source of ubiquitin-controlled ferroptotic iron in the reperfused brain. Li et al. have reported that USP14 directly deubiquitinates and stabilizes NCOA4, which enhances ferritinophagy and promotes the release of ferritin bound iron into the labile iron pool [33]. Through this pathway, ferritin stores are converted into redox active iron and neuronal fer- roptosis is amplified during cerebral I/R. These studies indicate that the ubiquitin system is not simply associated with iron overload during reperfusion. It directly controls how iron is taken up, retained, buffered, and released, and in this way influences ferroptosis in the ischemic heart and brain.
