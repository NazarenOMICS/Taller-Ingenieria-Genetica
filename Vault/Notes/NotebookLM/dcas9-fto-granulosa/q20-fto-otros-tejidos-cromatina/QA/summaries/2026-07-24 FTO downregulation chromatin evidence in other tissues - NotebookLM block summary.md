## Hilo central del bloque

- Based on the provided sources, **heart tissue (myocardium and cardiomyocytes)** is the primary non-granulosa tissue where oxidative stress has been reported to reduce FTO expression.
- Several of the provided sources examine or propose chromatin-level and regulatory DNA mechanisms to explain changes in FTO expression, particularly through **DNA methylation**, **transcription factor activity**, and **chromatin remodeling** driven by metabolic stress.
- In several non-granulosa stress models—most notably in cardiac and metabolic tissues—the regulation of **FTO** expression at the transcriptional and promoter levels involves specific transcription factors and metabolic feedback loops.
- Evidence for the **direct repression** of *FTO* through chromatin-level changes and transcription factor (TF) activity is documented in several non-granulosa stress models, though the sources acknowledge that much of the field still relies on associative mRNA/protein correlations  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 10|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q20-fto-otros-tejidos-cromatina/Sources/chen_2026_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt_sup_gt.pdf#Passage 7|[2]]].
- Based on the evidence from non-granulosa stress models, the following mechanisms are the most plausible to transfer into the granulosa oxidative-stress context, while others remain speculative due to lack of direct evidence or high tissue specificity.
- Significant research gaps remain before mechanisms of **FTO** regulation identified in tissues like the heart or brain can be definitively applied to granulosa cell aging. While the sources provide conceptual bridges, they highlight several critical areas where direct evidence is missing.
