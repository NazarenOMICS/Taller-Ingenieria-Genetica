# Q03 - According to these sources, once the dCas9-p300 effector is withdrawn or inactivated (e.g., via inducible/degron systems), how long does the induced gene activation persist before returning to baseline?

Based on the sources, the duration of gene activation following the withdrawal or inactivation of **dCas9-p300** is determined by the speed at which the effector is cleared from the cell and the relative lack of self-sustaining "epigenetic memory" associated with histone acetylation compared to other marks.

### Kinetics of Effector Withdrawal
When using inducible systems to inactivate the effector, the sources provide specific timelines for the disappearance of the dCas9 protein:
*   **Rapid Degradation:** In **Auxin-Inducible Degron (AID)** systems, the clearance of the dCas9-effector protein pool typically takes **1 to 2 hours**, with an estimated protein half-life of **less than 15 minutes**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/dirk_2017_drug_tunable_multidimensional_synthetic_gene_control_using_inducible.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]].
*   **Functional Abrogation:** Upon addition of the inducing agent (e.g., auxin) to trigger degradation, researchers observed a **"sharp drop"** in reporter output  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/dirk_2017_drug_tunable_multidimensional_synthetic_gene_control_using_inducible.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]]. In stable cell lines targeting endogenous genes, the transcriptional upregulation was **"largely abrogated"** when measured 48 hours after the induction of degradation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/dirk_2017_drug_tunable_multidimensional_synthetic_gene_control_using_inducible.pdf#Passage 3|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 4|[4]]].

### Persistence of Activation (Epigenetic Memory)
The sources distinguish between the immediate effect of the dCas9-p300 complex and the long-term persistence of the H3K27ac mark it deposits:
*   **Short-Term vs. Permanent Memory:** Unlike DNA methylation, which is associated with long-term "locked-in" states and permanent memory, histone modifications like acetylation are often characterized as providing **"short-term" or "flexible" epigenetic memory**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 5|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 6|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 2|[7]]].
*   **Requirement for Maintenance:** One source notes that it remains unclear whether regulatory activity is due to the induced epigenetic mark itself or the ongoing physical association of the dCas9-fusion complex with the target site  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 7|[8]]].
*   **Potential for Rapid Reversal:** For many histone-modifying systems, the sources suggest that a repressive or active state may be reversed **before one cell cycle is completed or within very few cell divisions** through passive dilution or the action of endogenous "eraser" enzymes (such as HDACs) once the synthetic "writer" is removed  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 6|[6]]].
*   **Contrast with Repressive Marks:** Systems that establish heterochromatin (like HP1$\alpha$ recruiting H3K9me3) can maintain silencing for **several weeks** and through multiple cell divisions after the initial stimulus is withdrawn  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 8|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 9|[10]]]. In contrast, the sources categorize histone deacetylation (the reciprocal of p300 activity) as having **"no memory,"** implying the active acetylated state likely returns to baseline quickly once the writer is inactivated  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 9|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]].

### Exceptions and "Protracted" Effects
While acetylation is generally transient, Source 7 suggests that under specific biological conditions—such as during the transition of T cells from an active to a resting state—interfering with the "gatekeeping" process of deacetylation can lead to **"protracted viral gene expression"** that remains significantly elevated **even 14 days after** the removal of an HDAC inhibitor  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 4|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 5|[13]]]. This suggests that if dCas9-p300 successfully "reprograms" a locus to prevent the establishment of a repressive state, the activation could potentially persist longer than expected for a simple histone mark  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 5|[13]]].
