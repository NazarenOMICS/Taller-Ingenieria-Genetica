# Q05 - According to these sources, does the H3K27ac mark itself decay at the same rate as the transcriptional activation once dCas9-p300 is removed, or can epigenetic memory persist after expression returns to baseline?

Based on the sources, the **H3K27ac mark is generally characterized as a transient and "flexible" modification** that decays relatively quickly once the synthetic writer (dCas9-p300) is removed, with transcriptional activation typically returning to baseline shortly thereafter  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/dirk_2017_drug_tunable_multidimensional_synthetic_gene_control_using_inducible.pdf#Passage 7|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 3|[2]]].

### Relative Decay and Kinetics
The sources do not provide high-resolution, side-by-side numerical decay curves comparing the H3K27ac mark to mRNA levels, but they offer insights into the timing of these processes:
*   **Rapid Effector Clearance:** In systems like the Auxin-Inducible Degron (AID), the dCas9-effector protein pool is cleared within **1 to 2 hours** of withdrawal, with a protein half-life of **less than 15 minutes**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/dirk_2017_drug_tunable_multidimensional_synthetic_gene_control_using_inducible.pdf#Passage 3|[3]]]. 
*   **Abrogation of Expression:** Following this rapid clearance, transcriptional upregulation of target genes (such as *RasL11a* and *Arpc1b*) was found to be **"largely abrogated"** when measured 48 hours after the start of degradation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 5|[4]]].
*   **Eraser Dominance:** The persistence of H3K27ac is limited by the constant activity of endogenous "eraser" enzymes (HDACs). One source explicitly categorizes histone deacetylation as having **"no memory,"** meaning the active state likely reverses **before one cell cycle is completed or within very few cell divisions** once the synthetic writer is no longer present to counteract erasers  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 3|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 7|[5]]].

### Persistence of Epigenetic Memory
While standard acetylation-mediated activation is considered short-lived, there are exceptions and theoretical frameworks for longer persistence:
*   **"Flexible" Memory:** Histone modifications are often described as providing **short-term or flexible memory** compared to more permanent marks like DNA methylation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 3|[2]]]. This flexibility allows for transient regulatory states that can be reversed by either erasers or passive dilution during cell division  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/minhee_2016_the_epigenome_the_next_substrate_for_engineering.pdf#Passage 3|[2]]].
*   **Blocking Repression:** There is evidence that altering acetylation can have more durable, **"protracted"** effects if it occurs during a critical biological "gateway." For example, interfering with deacetylation in T cells during their transition to a resting state led to expression levels that remained elevated **14 days after the removal of the stimulus**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 4|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]]. In this case, the persistence is not necessarily due to the mark itself but because the acetylation **blocked the establishment of permanent repressive marks** (like H3K9me3)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/jackson_2022_a_histone_deacetylase_network_regulates_epigenetic_reprogramming_and.pdf#Passage 6|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/daan_2017_dcas9_a_versatile_tool_for_epigenome_editing.pdf#Passage 4|[9]]].

### Limitations in Current Data
The sources highlight that identifying the exact causal relationship between the mark's decay and the return of expression to baseline remains a challenge:
*   **Complex vs. Mark:** Because the dCas9-p300 complex remains physically associated with the target site during the activation window, it is often **"unclear whether the regulatory activity is due to the induced epigenetic mark or the complex"** itself  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 2|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]].
*   **Need for Granular Research:** The sources note that identifying the "temporal kinetics of an epigenetic mark and its physical role on functional epigenetic memory" is a primary goal for future research using these rapid-degradation tools  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q02-h3k27ac-reversibilidad/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]].

> CRISPR-mediated epigenome editing The definition of “epigenetics” is heavily debated116,117. Here, we use the word “epigenetic” to imply the molecular mechanism of heritable gene expression changes that cannot be attributed to changes in DNA sequence information. Unlike epigenetics, which implies the mechanism, the epigenome describes all posttranslational modifications and other chromatin features associated with regulatory elements in the genome. Recent large-scale epigenomic efforts such as the Encyclopedia of DNA elements (ENCODE) and Roadmap Epigenome Mapping Consortium (REMC) efforts have mapped chromatin modifications both on DNA and histone proteins across the genome in various cell lines as well as primary cell types and tissues118,119. Although these epigenomic maps revealed unprecedented insight into cell-type-specific gene regulation and genome organization, the functional roles of various epigenomic features, such as histone modifications and DNA methylation, remain to be fully understood. To this end, locus-specific epigenome mapping tools and technologies are expected to greatly empower researchers to elucidate functional roles of chromatin modifications. Such tools will enable investigating some of the long-standing questions of chromatin biology such as the causal relationship between the presence of an epigenetic mark and gene expression. Further-more, the ability the alter locus-specific epigenetic marks may enable us to identify the temporal kinetics of an epigenetic mark and its physical role on the functional epigenetic memory and gene expression. Therefore, soon after the CRISPR-Cas9 system was harnessed as an efficient gene-editing technology, researchers used the programmable capacity of dCas9 to recruit various epigenetic writers and erasers to a specific locus.
