# Q06 - What gaps remain in these sources regarding directly quantifying or bounding the risk of supraphysiological FTO activation specifically via dCas9-p300 CRISPRa in granulosa cells?

Based on the provided sources, several significant gaps remain regarding the direct quantification or bounding of the risk of **supraphysiological FTO activation** specifically via **dCas9-p300** CRISPRa in **granulosa cells**:

### **1. Lack of Primary Data on Granulosa Cells**
While the sources mention FTO's role in various tissues—such as the heart  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/dipanjana_2026_fto_dependent_m6a_rna_dysregulation_underlies_memory_deficits.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]], hippocampus  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/dipanjana_2026_fto_dependent_m6a_rna_dysregulation_underlies_memory_deficits.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/xingxing_2023_overexpression_of_fto_inhibits_excessive_proliferation_and_promotes.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]], and renal mesangial cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/xingxing_2023_overexpression_of_fto_inhibits_excessive_proliferation_and_promotes.pdf#Passage 8|[4]]]—they contain **virtually no primary experimental data on granulosa cells**. The only mention of this cell type appears in a bibliography reference titled *"The m6A mRNA demethylase FTO in granulosa cells retards FOS-dependent ovarian aging,"* cited within the paper by Xingxing et al.  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/julie_2023_gene_modulation_with_crispr_based_tools_in_human.pdf#Passage 2|[5]]]. Consequently, the sources do not provide:
*   Baseline physiological levels of FTO in granulosa cells.
*   The specific transcriptomic or metabolic consequences of FTO activation within this specific cell population.

### **2. Absence of Specific Efficacy Data for dCas9-p300 on FTO**
The sources discuss various CRISPRa effectors, such as **SAM, VPR, and SunTag**, highlighting their ability to drive high levels of expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/neal_2020_defining_the_atpome_reveals_cross_optimization_of_metabolic.pdf#Passage 3|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/julie_2023_gene_modulation_with_crispr_based_tools_in_human.pdf#Passage 7|[7]]]. However, **dCas9-p300** is categorized more broadly as an **epigenome editing tool**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/julie_2023_gene_modulation_with_crispr_based_tools_in_human.pdf#Passage 8|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/julie_2023_gene_modulation_with_crispr_based_tools_in_human.pdf#Passage 9|[9]]]. 
*   **Modest Induction Gap:** One source notes that chromatin editors like dCas9-p300 often lead to **"modest gene expression changes"** compared to the high-potency CRISPRa effectors like SAM or VPR  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/paz_2021_the_role_of_m6a_m5c_and_rna_modifications.pdf#Passage 3|[10]]].
*   **Quantification Gap:** There is no data in the sources quantifying the specific fold-change dCas9-p300 can achieve for the *FTO* locus. Without this, it is impossible to determine if this specific tool naturally "bounds" the risk by producing only modest increases or if it can still push FTO into a supraphysiological range.

### **3. Undefined "Risk Boundaries" for FTO Levels**
While the sources identify the *types* of risks associated with FTO dysregulation (e.g., **oncogenic transformation** in leukemia  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/xiulin_2021_the_role_of_m6a_modification_in_the_biological.pdf#Passage 6|[11]]], metabolic shifts in **adipogenesis**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/beijian_2021_m6a_demethylase_fto_attenuates_cardiac_dysfunction_by_regulating.pdf#Passage 2|[12]]], and **cardiac energy supply** disruption  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/dipanjana_2026_fto_dependent_m6a_rna_dysregulation_underlies_memory_deficits.pdf#Passage 7|[13]]]), they do not establish **quantitative thresholds** for these risks.
*   **Threshold Gap:** The sources do not define at what exact fold-increase (e.g., 2-fold vs. 10-fold) FTO transitions from a "stabilizer" of function to a "driver" of disease or cancer. 
*   **Context Gap:** Because FTO acts as a global orchestrator of the m6A landscape  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/alain_2021_orthogonal_control_of_mean_and_variability_of_endogenous.pdf#Passage 2|[14]]], its "safe" physiological range likely varies by tissue. The sources provide no information on what this range is for granulosa cells.

### **4. Limited Monitoring of Multi-System Pleiotropy**
The sources suggest strategies like **TuNR** for titrating expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/paz_2021_the_role_of_m6a_m5c_and_rna_modifications.pdf#Passage 8|[15]]] and **Direct RNA sequencing** for monitoring m6A patterns  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/julie_2023_gene_modulation_with_crispr_based_tools_in_human.pdf#Passage 5|[16]]], but they do not apply these to bound the risk of FTO activation.
*   **Experimental Bridge Gap:** There is no study in the sources that combines **dCas9-p300** activation with systemic monitoring (like **Perturb-seq**) to observe the "pleiotropic burden" of over-activating FTO  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q13-riesgo-desregulacion-fto/Sources/neal_2020_defining_the_atpome_reveals_cross_optimization_of_metabolic.pdf#Passage 4|[17]]]. 
*   **Concordance Gap:** One source notes a lack of concordance between CRISPRi and CRISPRa hits, suggesting that simply increasing the expression of energy-regulating genes does not always further increase output [18]. This suggests the risk of FTO activation might not be linear, a phenomenon not yet quantified for dCas9-p300 in these sources.

In summary, while the sources describe FTO’s general risks and the theoretical mechanics of dCas9-p300, they lack the **integrative experimental data** necessary to define a safe upper bound for FTO activation in the specific context of granulosa cells.
