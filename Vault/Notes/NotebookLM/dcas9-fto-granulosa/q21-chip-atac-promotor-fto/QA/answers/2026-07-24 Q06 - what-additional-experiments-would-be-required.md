# Q06 - What additional experiments would be required in granulosa cells before treating the public ChIP-seq/ATAC-seq signals as evidence for the mechanism of FTO downregulation?

While public ChIP-seq and ATAC-seq signals from repositories like ENCODE provide a foundational map of the ***FTO*** regulatory environment, treating these signals as definitive evidence for a mechanism of downregulation in granulosa cells (GCs) requires moving from **predicted correlations** to **causal validation**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/e_2021_disease_associated_genetic_variants_in_the_regulatory_regions.pdf#Passage 9|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 12|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 15|[3]]]. 

The following additional experiments are required to substantiate these mechanisms in the specific context of GC biology:

### **1. Causal Validation via CRISPR/Cas9 Editing**
Public datasets often highlight correlations between SNPs (like **rs1421085**) and chromatin accessibility, but they do not prove that these variants cause expression changes in GCs  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/paola_2018_using_three_dimensional_regulatory_chromatin_interactions_from_adult.pdf#Passage 4|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 16|[5]]].
*   **Enhancer/Promoter Deletion:** Use CRISPR to delete or mutate the predicted regulatory elements (e.g., intronic enhancers) in human **KGN cells** or **primary GCs** to observe the effect on endogenous ***FTO*** mRNA and protein levels  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/carrasco_2026_novel_genome_editing_approaches_to_manipulate_apical_meristem.pdf#Passage 1|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/yongli_2024_pan_cistrome_analysis_of_the_leaf_accessible_chromatin.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/carrasco_2026_novel_genome_editing_approaches_to_manipulate_apical_meristem.pdf#Passage 2|[8]]].
*   **Base Editing:** Specifically mutate the risk/protective alleles of candidate SNPs within GCs to determine if they are sufficient to alter ***FTO*** transcription  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/carrasco_2026_novel_genome_editing_approaches_to_manipulate_apical_meristem.pdf#Passage 2|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 13|[9]]].

### **2. Functional Validation of Allelic Activity**
Predictions from deep learning models (like TREDNet) regarding allele-specific enhancer activity are considered **nominations** and require bench testing  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 17|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/anthony_2018_shared_genetic_contribution_to_type_1_and_type.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]].
*   **Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assays:** Clone the specific DNA sequences containing risk and protective alleles (e.g., at the ***FTO*** promoter or Intron 1) into reporter vectors and transfect them into GCs to confirm that the alleles drive differential transcriptional activity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/anthony_2018_shared_genetic_contribution_to_type_1_and_type.pdf#Passage 4|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/yunxia_2021_a_compendium_and_comparative_epigenomics_analysis_of_cis.pdf#Passage 3|[13]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 18|[14]]].
*   **Massively Parallel Reporter Assays (MPRA):** While some MPRA data exists for adipocytes and fetal brain, conducting GC-specific MPRAs would establish which variants in the ***FTO*** locus are functional in a reproductive context  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 19|[15]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 6|[16]]].

### **3. Validation of Transcription Factor (TF) Recruitment**
Many TFs (e.g., **ONECUT2**, **ARID5B**, **AR**) are predicted to bind the ***FTO*** locus based on motifs or proxy cell types  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 8|[17]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/jiang_2025_fto_mediated_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt.pdf#Passage 1|[18]]].
*   **ChIP-qPCR or CUT&RUN in Primary GCs:** Perform targeted binding assays for predicted repressors (like **ONECUT2**) or activators specifically in GCs, as TF binding is highly tissue-specific  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 7|[19]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 3|[20]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/madhusudhan_2019_atac_seq_reveals_alterations_in_open_chromatin_in.pdf#Passage 6|[21]]].
*   **TF Perturbation:** Perform **siRNA/shRNA knockdown** or **overexpression** of candidate TFs and measure the resulting changes in ***FTO*** expression to confirm their role as regulatory drivers  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 8|[22]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 9|[23]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/jiang_2025_fto_mediated_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt.pdf#Passage 2|[24]]].
*   **Co-Immunoprecipitation (Co-IP):** If the mechanism involves TFs recruiting other machinery (like **KAT8** or **DNMTs**), Co-IP is needed to confirm these protein-protein interactions within GC nuclei  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/jiang_2025_fto_mediated_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt.pdf#Passage 3|[25]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/jiang_2025_fto_mediated_m_lt_sup_gt_6_lt.pdf#Passage 4|[26]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 20|[27]]].

### **4. Context-Specific 3D Chromatin Mapping**
Sources note that mechanisms involving distal enhancers (like those targeting *IRX3/5*) rely on 3D contacts that are often mapped in proxies like **BMECs** or **HUVECs**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/carrasco_2026_novel_genome_editing_approaches_to_manipulate_apical_meristem.pdf#Passage 3|[28]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/paola_2018_using_three_dimensional_regulatory_chromatin_interactions_from_adult.pdf#Passage 1|[29]]].
*   **GC-Specific Hi-C or 3C:** Generate high-resolution **3D chromatin interaction maps** (e.g., Hi-C or Promoter-Capture Hi-C) directly in primary GCs across different maturation stages to confirm the physical interaction between ***FTO*** regulatory regions and its promoter  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/srivastava_2026_regulatory_risk_loci_link_disrupted_androgen_response_to.pdf#Passage 15|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/yang_2026_the_orchestrated_interplay_between_dna_methylation_and_n6.pdf#Passage 5|[30]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/carrasco_2026_novel_genome_editing_approaches_to_manipulate_apical_meristem.pdf#Passage 4|[31]]].

### **5. Epigenetic Perturbation of DNA Methylation**
Since DNA methylation at the ***FTO*** promoter is a known mechanism of silencing, its contribution in GCs must be tested  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/carrasco_2026_novel_genome_editing_approaches_to_manipulate_apical_meristem.pdf#Passage 5|[32]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/paper_2024_eacr_2024_innovative_cancer_science_10_13_june.pdf#Passage 4|[33]]].
*   **Demethylation Assays:** Treat GCs with **DNMT inhibitors** (like 5-azacytidine) or use **CRISPR-dCas9 targeted demethylation** (e.g., SunTag-TET1cd) to determine if removing promoter methylation restores ***FTO*** expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/e_2021_disease_associated_genetic_variants_in_the_regulatory_regions.pdf#Passage 3|[34]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/yang_2026_epigenetic_regulation_of_uterine_smooth_muscle_tumors_histone.pdf#Passage 1|[35]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/yang_2026_the_orchestrated_interplay_between_dna_methylation_and_n6.pdf#Passage 4|[36]]].
*   **Bisulfite Sequencing:** Use **Whole-Genome Bisulfite Sequencing (WGBS)** or **RRBS** specifically on GCs from different disease states (e.g., PCOS vs. control) to correlate methylation levels with ***FTO*** downregulation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 10|[37]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 11|[38]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 5|[39]]].

### **6. Dynamic Developmental Profiling**
Because GCs undergo rapid remodeling during maturation, static datasets may miss the critical window of downregulation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q21-chip-atac-promotor-fto/Sources/zhang_2025_fosl2_facilitates_chromatin_accessibility_to_determine_developmental_events.pdf#Passage 12|[40]]].
*   **Time-Course ATAC-seq/RNA-seq:** Profile GCs at multiple intervals during the transition from **antral** to **ovulatory** phases to establish the exact temporal sequence of chromatin closure and ***FTO*** repression [42, 43].

> Abstract A major challenge in deciphering the complex genetic landscape of polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) lies in the limited understanding of how susceptibility loci drive molecular mechanisms across diverse phenotypes. To address this, we integrated molecular and epigenomic annotations from proposed causal cell types and employed a deep learning (DL) framework to predict cell type–specific regulatory effects of PCOS-risk variants. Our analysis revealed that these variants affect key transcription factor–binding sites, including NR4A1/2, NHLH2, FOXA1, and WT1, which regulate gonadotropin signaling, folliculogenesis, and steroidogenesis across brain and endocrine cell types. The DL model, which showed strong concordance with reporter assay data, identified enhancer-disrupting activity in ∼20% of risk variants. Notably, many of these variants disrupt transcription factors involved in androgen-mediated signaling, providing molecular insights into hyperandrogenemia in PCOS. Variants prioritized by the model were more pleiotropic and exerted stronger regulatory effects on gene expression compared with other risk variants. Using the IRX3-FTO locus as a case study, we demonstrate how regulatory disruptions in tissues such as the fetal brain, pancreas, adipocytes, and endothelial cells may link obesity- associated mechanisms to PCOS pathogenesis via neuronal development, metabolic dysfunction, and impaired folliculogenesis. Collectively, our findings highlight the utility of integrating DL models with epigenomic data to uncover disease-relevant variants, reveal cross-tissue regulatory effects, and refine mechanistic understanding of PCOS.
