# Q05 - According to these sources (e.g., CRISPRa screening libraries), what controls are used at the screening/library scale to distinguish true hits from artifacts?

At the screening and library scale, CRISPRa experiments utilize several layers of controls to distinguish true biological hits from artifacts such as off-target toxicity, system-induced stress, and statistical noise.

### 1. sgRNA-Level Controls
*   **Non-targeting Controls (NTCs):** Genome-wide libraries like **Calabrese (CRISPRa)** and **Dolcetto (CRISPRi)** include a standard set of approximately **992 to 1,000 non-targeting sgRNAs**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 16|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]]. These sequences are designed not to match any genomic site and serve as a baseline for determining the "neutral" effect of the dCas9-activator complex on cell fitness  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 16|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/albert_2013_multiplexed_activation_of_endogenous_genes_by_crispr_on.pdf#Passage 7|[3]]].
*   **Safe-targeting Controls:** Unlike NTCs, these guides are filtered to target **non-functional genomic regions**. They control for the physiological impact of dCas9 binding to DNA without perturbing gene expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/yang_2019_engineered_crispra_enables_programmable_eukaryote_like_gene_activation.pdf#Passage 4|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/yang_2019_engineered_crispra_enables_programmable_eukaryote_like_gene_activation.pdf#Passage 2|[5]]].
*   **Mismatch or Mutant sgRNAs:** In both mammalian and bacterial screens, researchers use "mismatch" sgRNAs (e.g., random spacers or mutated versions of target guides like `sgTetO-mut`) to establish the baseline "OFF" state for calculating the system's dynamic range and ensuring activation is sequence-specific  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 4|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 5|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 17|[8]]].

### 2. Statistical Controls and Hit Calling
*   **Grouping Into "Dummy Genes":** To calculate p-values and false discovery rates (FDR), non-targeting control sgRNAs are often randomly grouped into **"dummy genes"** of the same size as the targeted gene sets (e.g., sets of 3, 4, or 6 guides)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/albert_2013_multiplexed_activation_of_endogenous_genes_by_crispr_on.pdf#Passage 7|[3]]]. This allows researchers to measure how often a random set of guides might show a phenotype by chance  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/albert_2013_multiplexed_activation_of_endogenous_genes_by_crispr_on.pdf#Passage 7|[3]]].
*   **Requirement for Concordant Evidence:** A core principle in CRISPRa screening is that a true hit must be driven by **multiple independent sgRNAs** targeting the same locus  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 8|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 10|[10]]]. If a phenotype is observed for only a single sgRNA while other high-specificity guides targeting the same gene fail to show an effect, the hit is likely an artifact of off-target activity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 8|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]].

### 3. Pre-emptive Specificity Filtering
*   **GuideScan Specificity Scores:** Modern libraries use data-driven models like the **GuideScan-aggregated CFD score** to filter out sgRNAs with high off-target potential  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 12|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 18|[13]]]. Sources indicate that filtering for sgRNAs with a GuideScan score **above 0.2** is essential for removing "confounded" hits in growth screens, where off-target binding—rather than gene activation—causes cellular toxicity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/josh_2019_mitigation_of_off_target_toxicity_in_crispr_cas9.pdf#Passage 12|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/kendall_2018_up_down_and_out_optimized_libraries_for_crispra.pdf#Passage 4|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/kendall_2018_up_down_and_out_optimized_libraries_for_crispra.pdf#Passage 6|[15]]].
*   **Sequence Filtering:** Libraries are further refined by removing sgRNAs with extreme GC content (e.g., <20% or >80%) or sequences that might interfere with expression, such as poly-T tracts or specific restriction sites  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/albert_2013_multiplexed_activation_of_endogenous_genes_by_crispr_on.pdf#Passage 4|[16]]].

### 4. Orthogonal and Global Validation Readouts
*   **Comparison to ORF Libraries:** CRISPRa hits are often cross-validated against **Open Reading Frame (ORF) overexpression libraries**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/albert_2013_multiplexed_activation_of_endogenous_genes_by_crispr_on.pdf#Passage 3|[17]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/yang_2019_engineered_crispra_enables_programmable_eukaryote_like_gene_activation.pdf#Passage 5|[18]]]. While the technologies are not fully interchangeable, the overlap in top hits (such as *EGFR* or *RAF1* in drug resistance screens) provides high confidence in the biological relevance of those genes  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/yang_2019_engineered_crispra_enables_programmable_eukaryote_like_gene_activation.pdf#Passage 5|[18]]].
*   **Transcriptome-wide Profiling:** Large-scale experiments often include **microarray or RNA-seq** analysis of hit populations to confirm that the CRISPRa system induces the intended target without causing massive, unintended perturbations in the global transcriptome  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q11-controles-experimentales/Sources/yang_2019_engineered_crispra_enables_programmable_eukaryote_like_gene_activation.pdf#Passage 6|[19]]].
*   **Dose-Response Tuning:** In some systems, the expression of dCas9 or the effector can be tuned via inducible promoters. True hits should show a proportional relationship between the level of system induction and the resulting phenotype [21, 22].

> We questioned whether these off-target growth effects were purely a function of the absolute number of off-target sites or specific to a subset of off-target sites. We and others have shown that, in the context of coding gene screens, the number of perfect matches or 1-mismatch off-targets correlates with growth phenotypes27,28. However, the analyses presented here do not include any sgRNAs with perfect genomic matches at any other place in the genome, nor sgRNAs with 1-mismatch off-targets. Across all four CRISPR-Cas9 platforms used in the tiling screens, the GuideScan score was predictive of off-target effects on cell fitness (Fig. 3c and Supplementary Fig. 6A), yet there was very weak correlation between growth effects and the absolute number of off-target sites (with 2 or 3 mismatches each), especially for CRISPRi/a (Supplementary Fig. 6B, C). Indeed, some outlier sgRNAs with thousands of off-target sites had no effects on growth. Thus, when designing and interpreting screens, the propensity to bind or cut as captured by the specificity score should be considered, rather than simply the number of off-target binding locations. These propensities are predicted for each off-target location by the CFD score44 as a weighted function of the mismatch number, position, and nucleotide identity, and then aggregated across all off-target locations into a GuideScan aggregate specificity score. Lastly, the optimal GuideScan score cutoff for filtering out false positives while retaining library density varies slightly but is approximately 0.2 for CRISPRi/a and Cas9 (Supplemen-tary Fig. 6D).
