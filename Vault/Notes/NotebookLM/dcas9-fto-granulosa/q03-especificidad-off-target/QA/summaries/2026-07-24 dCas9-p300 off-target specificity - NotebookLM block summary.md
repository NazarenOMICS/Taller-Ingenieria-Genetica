## Hilo central del bloque

- Based on the provided sources, **dCas9-p300** (a fusion of nuclease-dead Cas9 and the catalytic core of the human p300 acetyltransferase) exhibits a distinct specificity profile compared to wild-type (WT) Cas9, characterized by its focus on **epigenetic modification rather than permanent sequence alteration.**
- Based on the provided sources, dCas9-based systems are predisposed to unintended binding or activation due to specific guide RNA (sgRNA) mismatch tolerances, positional sensitivities along the guide sequence, and broader genomic or structural features.
- The sources describe several genome-wide profiling methods for assessing the **DNA binding specificity** of dCas9 and the **functional transcriptomic consequences** of CRISPRa systems. These methods differentiate between where a dCas9 complex physically associates with the genome and where it successfully induces changes in gene expression or the epigenome.
- The specificity profile of **dCas9 fusion-based CRISPR activators (CRISPRa)** differs from **catalytically active Cas9 nuclease editing** primarily in the nature of the genomic modification, the resulting cellular toxicity, and the stringency of the requirements for functional activity versus physical binding.
- While the sources characterize **dCas9-p300** as a potent epigenetic editor, they reveal several significant gaps and limitations regarding direct experimental evidence of its specificity in human cells, particularly when compared to the broader body of literature on general CRISPR-Cas9 off-targets.
