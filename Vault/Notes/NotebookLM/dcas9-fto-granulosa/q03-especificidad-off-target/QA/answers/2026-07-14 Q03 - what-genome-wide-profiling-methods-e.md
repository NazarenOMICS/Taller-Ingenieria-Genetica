# Q03 - What genome-wide profiling methods (e.g., ChIP-seq, CUT&RUN, GUIDE-seq, RNA-seq) do these sources describe for assessing dCas9 or CRISPRa binding specificity and global transcriptomic off-target effects?

The sources describe several genome-wide profiling methods for assessing the binding specificity and functional transcriptomic consequences of dCas9 and CRISPRa systems. These methods are categorized into those that detect physical binding, those that measure transcriptomic changes, and those that profile off-target epigenetic modifications.

### **Genome-Wide Binding Specificity**
*   **ChIP-seq (Chromatin Immunoprecipitation sequencing):** This is the primary method described for mapping the **DNA binding sites** of catalytically inactive **dCas9** genome-wide  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 3|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]]. It identifies where the dCas9 complex physically associates with the genome without introducing double-strand breaks (DSBs)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 4|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 2|[4]]]. One source notes that while dCas9 may associate with many genomic loci, these binding events do not always result in functional consequences like gene activation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 4|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 2|[4]]].
*   **DNase-seq and ATAC-seq:** These methods identify regions of **open chromatin** (chromatin accessibility)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]]. The sources highlight that dCas9 off-target binding is heavily enriched in these regions; therefore, integrating DNase-seq or ATAC-seq data allows for better in silico prediction of potential off-target binding sites  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]].

### **Global Transcriptomic Off-Target Effects**
*   **RNA-seq (RNA sequencing):** This method is used to comprehensively profile the **functional specificity** of CRISPRa systems by identifying **differentially expressed genes (DEGs)**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 3|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/hongyi_2020_applications_of_genome_editing_technology_in_the_targeted.pdf#Passage 3|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 4|[8]]]. By comparing the transcriptome of cells treated with a specific guide RNA to no-guide controls, researchers can determine if unintended genes are being transcriptionally activated  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/hongyi_2020_applications_of_genome_editing_technology_in_the_targeted.pdf#Passage 3|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 5|[9]]]. 
*   **WGS (Whole Genome Sequencing):** While more expensive and typically used to identify permanent mutations (indels), WGS can be used to compare sequences before and after treatment to uncover desired and unwanted editing events, though its sensitivity for low-frequency sites depends on sequencing depth  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 4|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 5|[10]]].

### **Epigenomic Off-Target Effects**
For dCas9-effector fusions (like dCas9-p300 or dCas9-methyltransferases), specific methods are used to profile "global epigenetic footprints":
*   **WGBS (Whole-genome bisulfite sequencing):** This is used to profile **genome-wide DNA methylation** levels to understand the off-target activity of dCas9-fused epigenetic editors  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 6|[12]]]. For example, it can detect if a dCas9-methyltransferase is leaving global methylation footprints independent of the sgRNA used  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]].
*   **ChIP-seq (Histone Marks):** This variation of ChIP-seq is used to evaluate off-target effects at the **epigenome level**, such as assessing unintended changes in histone acetylation (e.g., **H3K27ac**) induced by fusions like dCas9-p300  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 7|[13]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 8|[14]]].

### **Methods for Cleavage-Based Specificity (Cas9-Nuclease)**
While the query focuses on dCas9, the sources mention several "gold standard" methods for assessing the specificity of Cas9 nucleases (which create DSBs). These are often the technological precursors to specificity assessment in dCas9 systems:
*   **GUIDE-seq, Digenome-seq, CIRCLE-seq, and SITE-seq:** These are sensitive methods for the genome-wide, unbiased identification of **DSBs**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 3|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 9|[15]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 10|[16]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 11|[17]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 12|[18]]].
*   **BLISS and BLESS:** These methods capture and label DNA breaks **in situ** in fixed cells [19, 20].
*   **Discover-seq:** This method utilizes the endogenous DNA repair protein **MRE11** to identify in situ Cas off-targets via ChIP-seq [21].

**Note on CUT&RUN:** While you mentioned CUT&RUN as an example, the provided sources do not explicitly describe this specific method for assessing dCas9 or CRISPRa specificity. They rely primarily on ChIP-seq and RNA-seq for these purposes.

> Another more popular method to detect off-target sites in cells is called GUIDE-seq (Tsai et al., 2015). This technique relies on the delivery of double-stranded oligonucleotides (dsODNs) with known sequences, which can integrate into DSBs during NHEJ (nonhomologous end joining). The integrated dsODNs provide templates for targeted PCR amplification and sequencing of the tagged DNA fragments (Tsai et al., 2015; Malinin et al., 2021; Yaish et al., 2022) (Figure 1B). GUIDE-seq can detect off-target sites with indel frequencies as low as 0.03% (Tsai et al., 2015). GUIDE-seq is more sensitive than the IDLV method because dsODNs integrate more efficiently and precisely into DSBs, while the integration events of IDLV is low in number and can distribute as far as 500bp away from the actual DSB sites (Tsai et al., 2015; Cromer et al., 2023). A primary limitation of GUIDE-seq is relevant to the low delivery efficiency of dsODNs into cells, which results in detection of only 30%–50% of all the DSBs (Tsai et al., 2015; Pan et al., 2022).

Alternatively, DSB can be directly detected by BLESS (direct in situ breaks labeling, enrichment on streptavidin and next-generation sequencing), which captures DSBs in situ via the ligation of biotinylated linkers to cleavage sites in fixed cells (Crosetto et al., 2013) (Figure 1D). > To enhance the sensitivity of BLESS and reduce its requirement on cell number, BLISS (breaks labeling in situ and sequencing) technology was developed (Yan et al., 2017). BLISS ligates DSB ends with adapters containing the T7 promoter, so the tagged DNA fragments can be linearly amplified via T7-mediated transcription before sequencing (Yan et al., 2017; Ballarino et al., 2021). Compared to BLESS, BLISS demands only a few thousand cells and demonstrates a higher sensitivity. For example, Winston et al. performed side-by-side comparison between BLISS and BLESS to detect the off-target sites of validated sgRNAs targeting EMX1 or VEGFA (Yan et al., 2017). For the sgRNA targeting EMX1, BLESS uncovered 6 off-target sites, all of which are included in the 10 genuine off-target sites that BLISS discovered. Similarly, for the sgRNA targeting VEGFA, besides the 16 off-target sites that were detected by both methods, BLISS identified 27 additional offtarget sites that are not found by BLESS (Yan et al., 2017). Thus the sensitivity of BLISS is more than two folds higher than BLESS.
