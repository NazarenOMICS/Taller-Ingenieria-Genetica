# Q02 - What guide RNA mismatch tolerance or sequence features have been reported in these sources that predispose dCas9-based systems to bind or activate unintended genomic loci?

Based on the provided sources, dCas9-based systems are predisposed to unintended binding or activation due to specific guide RNA (sgRNA) mismatch tolerances, positional sensitivities along the guide sequence, and broader genomic or structural features.

### **Guide RNA Mismatch Tolerance**
*   **Quantitative Threshold:** Catalytically active Cas9 (the foundation for dCas9 fusions) is reported to tolerate up to **3 to 5 mismatches** between the sgRNA and the genomic DNA  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/congting_2023_off_target_effects_in_crispr_cas9_gene_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/fatemeh_2019_crispr_cpf1_proteins_structure_function_and_implications_for.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]].
*   **Less Stringent Binding:** Sources note that the requirements for physical DNA binding by dCas9 are **"less stringent"** than those for the catalytic cleavage performed by wild-type Cas9  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]]. Consequently, dCas9 can associate with a significantly higher number of genomic sites than a standard nuclease would successfully cut  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]].
*   **Seed Region Sensitivity:** The "seed sequence," typically defined as the **10 to 12 base pairs proximal to the PAM**, is critical for initial binding  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/tianxiang_2023_crispr_cas9_therapeutics_progress_and_prospects.pdf#Passage 5|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/tianxiang_2023_crispr_cas9_therapeutics_progress_and_prospects.pdf#Passage 6|[5]]]. Mismatches in this region are generally poorly tolerated and significantly reduce or abolish dCas9 association  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/fatemeh_2019_crispr_cpf1_proteins_structure_function_and_implications_for.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/tianxiang_2023_crispr_cas9_therapeutics_progress_and_prospects.pdf#Passage 7|[6]]].
*   **PAM-Distal Tolerance:** Mismatches at the distal end of the sgRNA (farthest from the PAM) are tolerated much more readily  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/fatemeh_2019_crispr_cpf1_proteins_structure_function_and_implications_for.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/tianxiang_2023_crispr_cas9_therapeutics_progress_and_prospects.pdf#Passage 7|[6]]]. Mismatches at the **18th to 20th base positions** can form an "unusual duplex conformation" or a **"Ruv loop structure"** that stabilizes the off-target complex rather than causing it to detach  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/tianxiang_2023_crispr_cas9_therapeutics_progress_and_prospects.pdf#Passage 7|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/hongyi_2020_applications_of_genome_editing_technology_in_the_targeted.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]].

### **Sequence and Structural Features**
*   **Target Homology:** Unintended activity primarily occurs at genomic sites that exhibit **high homology** to the intended target sequence  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/kendall_2018_optimized_libraries_for_crispr_cas9_genetic_screens_with.pdf#Passage 4|[8]]].
*   **The "Core" Sequence:** One source identifies a specific 4-nucleotide **"core" sequence** (located at the **+4 to +7 positions upstream of the PAM**) that appears to be particularly sensitive to mismatches  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/fatemeh_2019_crispr_cpf1_proteins_structure_function_and_implications_for.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]].
*   **TracrRNA Modifications:** Using modified tracrRNA scaffolds to improve on-target potency (such as extending the tetraloop in **tracr-v2** or **tracr-v14**) has been shown to simultaneously **increase off-target activity** when using mismatched sgRNAs  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/kendall_2018_optimized_libraries_for_crispr_cas9_genetic_screens_with.pdf#Passage 5|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/kendall_2018_optimized_libraries_for_crispr_cas9_genetic_screens_with.pdf#Passage 6|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 1|[11]]].
*   **Bidirectional Promoters:** In CRISPRa systems, targeting a specific promoter can lead to the unintended upregulation of an adjacent gene if the target site is located within a **bidirectional promoter**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/kendall_2018_optimized_libraries_for_crispr_cas9_genetic_screens_with.pdf#Passage 1|[12]]].

### **Genomic Predispositions**
*   **Chromatin Accessibility:** Mapping of dCas9 binding sites (ChIP-seq) indicates that off-target associations are heavily **enriched in regions of open chromatin**, as identified by DNase-seq  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/kendall_2018_optimized_libraries_for_crispr_cas9_genetic_screens_with.pdf#Passage 2|[13]]]. Integrating chromatin accessibility data into prediction algorithms significantly improves the identification of these unintended sites  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/ashot_2019_site_specific_manipulation_of_arabidopsis_loci_using_crispr.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]].
*   **Copy Number Variation:** At loci that are **copy-number amplified**, dCas9-based systems may show increased association, although they avoid the "cutting-related toxicity" that active Cas9 induces at these sites  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/kendall_2018_optimized_libraries_for_crispr_cas9_genetic_screens_with.pdf#Passage 3|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/tianxiang_2023_crispr_cas9_therapeutics_progress_and_prospects.pdf#Passage 8|[15]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/tianxiang_2023_crispr_cas9_therapeutics_progress_and_prospects.pdf#Passage 9|[16]]].
*   **PAM Requirements:** While SpCas9 is limited by its requirement for an **NGG PAM**, engineering variants to recognize a broader range of PAMs (like the SpRY variant) can increase the number of targetable sites but often comes with a higher risk of off-target activity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/fatemeh_2019_crispr_cpf1_proteins_structure_function_and_implications_for.pdf#Passage 2|[17]]].

In contrast, **Cpf1 (Cas12a)-based systems** are reported to have a **lower threshold for mismatches** than Cas9 systems, as mismatches within any of the first 18 nucleotides in the target DNA are more effective at impeding Cpf1 activity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q03-especificidad-off-target/Sources/fatemeh_2019_crispr_cpf1_proteins_structure_function_and_implications_for.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]].

> Although CRISPR/Cas systems exhibit tremendous potential in translational medicine, off-target effects remain a major challenge (Fu et al., 2013; Hsu et al., 2013; Pacesa et al., 2022). The off-target effects occur when Cas9 acts on untargeted genomic sites and creates cleavages that may lead to adverse outcomes. The off-target sites are often sgRNA-dependent, since Cas9 is known to tolerate up to 3 mismatches between sgRNA and genomic DNA (Fu et al., 2013; Hsu et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2016a). In this scenario, in silico tools are useful to search for potential off-target sites in the whole genome and calculate the likelihood of an off-target editing (Naeem et al., 2020). Nevertheless, accumulative studies have proved that sgRNAindependent off-target effects also exist, urging unbiased experimental detection and validation (Richter et al., 2020; O’Geen et al., 2015). In this review, we summarize available methods for the assessment of off-target effects, indicating their advantages versus limitations. Some of these detection methods for off-targets prediction is applicable for other family of Cas nucleases, such as Cas12a (Cpf1), which also create DSBs on off-target sites (Kim et al., 2019). Furthermore, we discuss strategies to improve CRISPR/Cas9 specificity and to reduce undesired mutagenesis, which is crucial for their future application in gene therapy.
