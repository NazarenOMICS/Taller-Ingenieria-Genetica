# Q03 - What challenges do these sources describe in delivering large Cas9/dCas9-fusion payloads (e.g., dCas9-p300, ~5-6 kb) via lentivirus, nanoparticles, or mRNA/RNP compared to smaller CRISPR cargos?

The sources describe several unique challenges associated with delivering large **Cas9/dCas9-fusion payloads** (such as dCas9-p300 or base editors, which can reach 5–6 kb) compared to standard CRISPR cargos. These challenges primarily revolve around **packaging constraints**, **colloidal stability**, and **cellular barriers** to entry and escape.

### **Lentiviral Vector Challenges**
*   **Packaging Capacity:** While lentiviral vectors (LV) are described as a "good candidate" for large payloads because they have a high cargo capacity of approximately **8–10 kb**, they still face limitations  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 3|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 8|[3]]]. Standard SpCas9 (gene length ~4.2 kb) already consumes a large portion of the limit, and adding large fusion domains like p300 leaves very little room for required regulatory elements like promoters or multiple guide RNAs  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 9|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 1|[6]]].
*   **Off-Target Risk:** A significant drawback of using integrating lentiviral vectors for large payloads is that they provide **stable and long-lasting expression**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 12|[7]]]. While this is ideal for some gene therapies, it is considered "unfavorable" for CRISPR because prolonged exposure to the nuclease significantly increases the frequency of **off-target editing** compared to transient delivery formats  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 8|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 12|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 9|[8]]].
*   **Safety Concerns:** Integrating such large constructs into the host genome carries a risk of **insertional mutagenesis**, which can lead to carcinogenesis  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 3|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 13|[9]]].

### **Nanoparticle (LNP/Polymer) Challenges**
*   **Encapsulation Efficiency:** Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) and polymers complex their cargo via electrostatic interactions. However, for large **ribonucleoprotein (RNP)** complexes, the negative charge is not uniformly distributed over the surface, making it difficult to achieve efficient encapsulation compared to smaller, uniformly charged nucleic acids  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 14|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 15|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 16|[12]]].
*   **Colloidal Instability and Aggregation:** Large Cas9-RNP payloads are prone to **aggregation and sedimentation**, particularly at the higher concentrations required for *in vivo* studies  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 17|[13]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 18|[14]]]. In animal experiments, these large LNP-RNP aggregates were suspected to be a cause of **lethal toxicity** in mice  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 18|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 19|[15]]].
*   **Structural Impact:** The large size of the cargo affects the final structure, size, and charge of the nanoparticle. Because different cells have varying membrane permeabilities, these altered physical characteristics can limit the types of cells that can successfully take up the large payloads  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 15|[11]]].

### **mRNA and RNP Format Challenges**
*   **Cellular Entry Barriers:** The high molecular weight of the Cas9 protein (158.9–160 kDa) and the length of its gene (~4–4.5 kb) make it physically challenging for these payloads to cross the cell membrane and translocate into the nucleus  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 9|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 1|[16]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 3|[17]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 20|[18]]].
*   **Endosomal Escape:** Once internalized, large CRISPR payloads face a major bottleneck in escaping from endosomes. It is estimated that **often only 1% of the cargo** successfully reaches the cytosol to exert its function  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/namit_2021_mrna_vaccines_for_infectious_diseases_principles_delivery_and.pdf#Passage 1|[19]]].
*   **Enzymatic Degradation:** Large, fragile mRNA or protein molecules require high levels of protection. If the delivery vehicle does not fully encapsulate the payload (which is harder with large fusion proteins), the cargo is vulnerable to **premature degradation** by extracellular nucleases or proteases like trypsin  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 16|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 21|[20]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 22|[21]]].
*   **Kinetics vs. Payload Size:** Interestingly, while RNPs are "ready-made" and should be active earlier, one study found that **mRNA delivery actually resulted in a faster onset of gene correction** and higher overall efficiency, potentially because the smaller mRNA-LNP particles were taken up more effectively than larger RNP-LNP complexes  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 23|[22]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 24|[23]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 25|[24]]].

### **Comparison with Smaller CRISPR Cargos**
Unlike the large payloads discussed above, smaller CRISPR components (like SaCas9 or guide RNAs alone) can fit into **Adeno-associated viruses (AAV)**, which have a strict packaging limit of **<4.4–4.7 kb**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 2|[25]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 4|[26]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 5|[27]]]. To deliver larger fusions via AAV, researchers must use **split-Cas9 systems** or dual-vector strategies, which come at a significant cost to both delivery and DNA-cutting efficiency  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]].
