# Q04 - According to these sources, what transfection or transduction approaches have been used or would be expected to work in granulosa-derived cell lines (KGN, COV434) or hard-to-transfect primary/stem-like cells, and what perturbation or toxicity concerns are noted?

For granulosa-derived cells and other hard-to-transfect primary or stem-like populations, the sources identify **lentiviral transduction**, **magnetofection**, **electroporation**, and **exosome-based delivery** as primary approaches, each with distinct trade-offs regarding efficiency and cellular stress.

### **Approaches for Granulosa and Hard-to-Transfect Cells**

*   **Lentiviral Transduction:** 
    *   **Usage:** Lentiviral vectors are explicitly used to transduce **primary rat granulosa cells (GCs)** for studying signaling pathways like PKA-CQR  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/pawan_2016_protein_kinase_a_a_master_kinase_of_granulosa.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/pawan_2016_protein_kinase_a_a_master_kinase_of_granulosa.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/pawan_2016_protein_kinase_a_a_master_kinase_of_granulosa.pdf#Passage 3|[3]]]. They are also noted as a "good candidate" for other primary glial cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia) due to their high cargo capacity (~10 kb) and ability to transduce non-dividing cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 6|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 7|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 1|[6]]].
    *   **Efficiency:** In primary rat GCs, transduction was sufficient to drive consistent protein expression and mimic FSH-mediated signaling 24 hours post-transduction  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/pawan_2016_protein_kinase_a_a_master_kinase_of_granulosa.pdf#Passage 4|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/pawan_2016_protein_kinase_a_a_master_kinase_of_granulosa.pdf#Passage 5|[8]]].
*   **Magnetofection (PEI-coated MNPs):**
    *   **Usage:** Specifically developed to improve delivery into **"hard-to-transfect"** cells like **Human Foreskin Fibroblasts (HFF)**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 6|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 5|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 1|[11]]].
    *   **Efficiency:** Magnetofection achieved **28.2% efficiency in HFF cells**, which significantly outperformed standard lipofection (7.89%) for this difficult cell type  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 1|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 7|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 2|[13]]].
*   **Electroporation and Physical Methods:**
    *   **Usage:** Used for **hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells (HSPCs)** and B-cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 26|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 27|[15]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 10|[16]]].
    *   **Alternative:** Novel techniques like **TRIAMF** (membrane filtration) have been used for HSPCs to achieve similar efficiency to electroporation but with lower cytotoxicity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 27|[15]]].
*   **Exosomes and Hybrid Systems:**
    *   **Usage:** Engineered exosomes (e.g., CD9-HuR) and exosome-liposome hybrids have been used to transfect **mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs)**, which are notoriously difficult to manipulate using standard reagents  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 11|[17]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/parisa_2024_comparing_chemical_transfection_electroporation_and_lentiviral_vector_transduction.pdf#Passage 6|[18]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/parisa_2024_comparing_chemical_transfection_electroporation_and_lentiviral_vector_transduction.pdf#Passage 10|[19]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 8|[20]]].

### **Perturbation and Toxicity Concerns**

The sources highlight several critical concerns when using these methods in sensitive primary or stem-like populations:

*   **Physical Damage and Cell Death:** 
    *   **Electroporation** is described as highly toxic because it harms the cell membrane, often leading to **permanent permeabilization** or high mortality rates  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 26|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 9|[21]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 10|[22]]]. 
    *   In comparison, magnetofection using SPIONs is reported to be relatively biocompatible and non-toxic at optimized concentrations (IC50 ~20 pg)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 1|[23]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 3|[24]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 8|[25]]].
*   **Genotoxicity and Insertional Mutagenesis:** 
    *   **Lentiviral vectors** carry a significant risk of **insertional mutagenesis** because they integrate their genetic cargo into the host genome  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 28|[26]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 29|[27]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 30|[28]]]. This is particularly concerning for stem cells intended for therapeutic use, as it can lead to carcinogenesis  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 28|[26]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 29|[27]]].
*   **Immunogenicity and Innate Responses:**
    *   Viral vectors (AAV and LV) may trigger **innate and adaptive immune responses**, which can eliminate edited cells or prevent repeated dosing  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/parisa_2024_comparing_chemical_transfection_electroporation_and_lentiviral_vector_transduction.pdf#Passage 11|[29]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/parisa_2024_comparing_chemical_transfection_electroporation_and_lentiviral_vector_transduction.pdf#Passage 12|[30]]].
    *   Even non-viral lipid nanoparticles (LNPs) can be **highly inflammatory**; for instance, Cas9 mRNA-LNPs were found to trigger higher pro-inflammatory cytokine release (IFN-α, IL-12) compared to RNP-LNPs in some contexts  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/kasra_2020_exosomal_micrornas_derived_from_mesenchymal_stem_cells_cell.pdf#Passage 1|[31]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 7|[32]]].
*   **Chemical Toxicity:**
    *   **Polyethylenimine (PEI)**, while effective, exhibits dose-dependent cytotoxicity by **depolarizing mitochondria** and stimulating immune sensing pathways [34, 35].
*   **Phenotypic Alteration:**
    *   For stem cells, there is a risk that the delivery process or the stable expression of Cas9 could lead to **uncontrolled differentiation** or "malignant alteration," which is why exosome-based cell-free therapies are often proposed as a safer alternative [36, 37].

> Results and Discussion Signaling pathways activated by FSH and by PKA-CQR. The ideal experimental paradigm to estab- lish whether PKA is sufficient to activate intracellular signaling pathways that are associated with FSH stimulation of GC differentiation would be to conduct a detailed timecourse of phospho-protein expression in response to FSH and PKA-CQR. However, the inherent difficulty in comparing the intracellular signaling pathways activated by FSH versus PKA-CQR in GCs is that the time-course for FSH activation is rapid and transient such that maximal stimulation typically occurs within the first 20 min. and declines to near basal levels by 60 min.7. > Extracellular Vesicles-Based Delivery of Cas9/gRNA The greatest challenge in the clinical application of the CRISPR/ Cas9 system is the lack of a safe and effective delivery system. Exosomes are natural membrane-bound vesicles exhibiting the advantages of high biocompatibility and low immunogenicity. They can carry various biomolecules, including plasmids, proteins, siRNAs, and miRNAs, that could serve as cell-free therapies (Duan et al., 2020b; Liang et al., 2021b). Thus, engineered exosomes have been used for targeted drug delivery (Duan et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2021a). Our studies have already proven the potential of targeted exosomes for miRNA and small molecular delivery in cartilage repair (Duan et al., 2020a; Liang et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2021). The functional components of gRNA and Cas9 protein can be encapsulated in exosomes and they can transmit the gene-editing activity of the CRISPR/Cas9 system intracellularly. Exosomes carrying functional gRNA and Cas9 proteins can be produced using bioengineered Vero and CHO cells. Exosomes can also deliver gRNA and Cas9 proteins to the target cells and achieve functional delivery of the CRISPR/Cas9 system (Chen et al., 2019). Due to their excellent biocompatibility and the rapid degradation of gRNA and Cas9 proteins inside cells, the delivery of gRNA and Cas9 reduces the risks of off-target effects and integrating into the genome. Therefore, exosomes may be the safest and most effective delivery method for the CRISPR/Cas9 system.
