# Q02 - According to these sources, what delivery platforms (lipid nanoparticles, viral vectors, RNP) have been used specifically for dCas9-based (nuclease-dead) constructs, and what are their reported efficiencies?

Based on the provided sources, **dCas9 (nuclease-dead Cas9)** constructs have been delivered using a variety of platforms including **magnetofection, viral vectors, lipid-based systems, and engineered exosomes**. These constructs are primarily utilized for gene regulation (CRISPRi/a), epigenome editing, and imaging  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 10|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 3|[3]]].

### **Reported Delivery Platforms and Efficiencies**

*   **Magnetofection (Magnetic Nanoparticles):**
    *   **Platform:** Superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (SPIONs) coated with polyethylenimine (PEI) were used to deliver dCas9-containing plasmids  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 4|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 5|[5]]].
    *   **Efficiency:** This method achieved a transfection efficiency of **~85.7% in HEK-293 cells** and **28.2% in Human foreskin fibroblasts (HFF)**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 1|[6]]]. This was notably more efficient than standard lipofection for the harder-to-transfect HFF cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 1|[6]]].

*   **Viral Vectors (AAV and Lentiviral):**
    *   **Adeno-associated Virus (AAV):** Used extensively for delivering dCas9-based epigenome editors to the central nervous system (CNS)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 2|[7]]].
        *   **Efficiency:** Systemic delivery of an AAV1-PHP.B vector expressing CRISPRa (dead SaCas9 fused to activators) resulted in a **350–450% increase in fluorescence intensity** for a reporter gene in the ventral brain of mice  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 2|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 3|[8]]].
        *   **Efficiency:** Intravenous injection of AAV particles harboring dCas9-VPR achieved a **2- to 3-fold increase in the expression** of the *Scn1a* gene in specific neurons  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 2|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 3|[8]]].
    *   **Lentiviral Vectors (LV):** These have a large cargo capacity (~10 kb), which is advantageous for "all-in-one" systems carrying large dCas9 fusion proteins  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mazhar_2018_the_crispr_tool_kit_for_genome_editing_and.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/vasco_2021_delivery_platforms_for_crispr_cas9_genome_editing_of.pdf#Passage 1|[10]]].

*   **Chemical and Polymer-Based Platforms:**
    *   **Lipofection:** Using Lipofectamine 2000 for dCas9 plasmid delivery resulted in **83.2% efficiency in HEK-293 cells** but only **7.89% in HFF cells**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/mahdi_2021_delivery_of_dcas9_crispr_system_into_the_hard.pdf#Passage 1|[6]]].
    *   **Dendrimers:** PAMAM dendrimers have been used for "effective transfection" of CRISPR/dCas9 plasmids into MCF-7 human breast adenocarcinoma cells while maintaining low cytotoxicity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/johanna_1970_lipid_nanoparticle_mediated_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for.pdf#Passage 11|[11]]].
    *   **Multistage Delivery Nanoparticles (MDNP):** These were reported to facilitate efficient CRISPR/dCas9 activation and tumor growth suppression *in vivo*  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 5|[12]]].

*   **Exosomes:**
    *   **Platform:** Engineered exosomes (such as CD9-HuR functionalized versions) have been used to encapsulate dCas9 for RNA inhibition  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 6|[13]]].
    *   **Efficiency:** While specific percentage efficiencies for dCas9-loaded exosomes were not detailed in the same summary table as other platforms, they are noted for their high biocompatibility and ability to deliver functional CRISPR/dCas9 components to the liver  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 7|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q10-sistemas-entrega/Sources/li_2021_nanoparticle_delivery_of_crispr_cas9_for_genome_editing.pdf#Passage 8|[15]]].

> Extracellular Vesicles-Based Delivery of Cas9/gRNA The greatest challenge in the clinical application of the CRISPR/ Cas9 system is the lack of a safe and effective delivery system. Exosomes are natural membrane-bound vesicles exhibiting the advantages of high biocompatibility and low immunogenicity. They can carry various biomolecules, including plasmids, proteins, siRNAs, and miRNAs, that could serve as cell-free therapies (Duan et al., 2020b; Liang et al., 2021b). Thus, engineered exosomes have been used for targeted drug delivery (Duan et al., 2021; Liang et al., 2021a). Our studies have already proven the potential of targeted exosomes for miRNA and small molecular delivery in cartilage repair (Duan et al., 2020a; Liang et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2021). The functional components of gRNA and Cas9 protein can be encapsulated in exosomes and they can transmit the gene-editing activity of the CRISPR/Cas9 system intracellularly. Exosomes carrying functional gRNA and Cas9 proteins can be produced using bioengineered Vero and CHO cells. Exosomes can also deliver gRNA and Cas9 proteins to the target cells and achieve functional delivery of the CRISPR/Cas9 system (Chen et al., 2019). Due to their excellent biocompatibility and the rapid degradation of gRNA and Cas9 proteins inside cells, the delivery of gRNA and Cas9 reduces the risks of off-target effects and integrating into the genome. Therefore, exosomes may be the safest and most effective delivery method for the CRISPR/Cas9 system.
