# Q02 - Which DNMT or TET enzymes are implicated in regulating FTO promoter methylation, if any, and in what biological contexts?

While the sources identify **DNMTs** and **TETs** as the general enzymatic machinery responsible for the DNA methylation cycle, they **do not explicitly name a specific member** of either family (e.g., DNMT3A or TET2) as the direct regulator of the ***FTO* promoter**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 3|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 4|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/xiaofeng_2021_methylation_multiplicity_and_its_clinical_values_in_cancer.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[4]]]. Instead, the sources focus on the **biological consequences** and **environmental triggers** of *FTO* promoter methylation status.

The sources report *FTO* promoter methylation changes in the following biological contexts:

### 1. Obesity and Adipose Homeostasis
*   **Hypomethylation induced by Lifestyle:** Factors such as a **high-fat diet** or **physical inactivity** are reported to induce **hypomethylation** of the *FTO* promoter  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]]. This hypomethylation markedly upregulates *FTO* expression, which then modulates the downstream *IRX3/IRX5* gene network  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 3|[6]]]. This process impacts energy expenditure and adipocyte differentiation, ultimately increasing an individual’s risk of obesity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 3|[6]]].
*   **Silencing through Hypermethylation:** Conversely, **hypermethylation** of the *FTO* promoter is associated with a **reduction in obesity risk** because it silences *FTO* expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]].

### 2. Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)
*   **Intronic Regulatory Sites:** Studies have identified specific CpG sites, such as **rs1121980** located in the **first intron** of the *FTO* gene, that display significant **hypomethylation** in patients with T2DM compared to healthy controls  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 2|[7]]].
*   **Early Risk Marker:** This hypomethylation is considered an **early risk factor** rather than a consequence of the disease, as it has been observed in young individuals who later developed T2DM  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 1|[8]]]. The site (rs1121980) is noted to colocalize with binding sites for **methylation-sensitive transcriptional regulators** and enhancers, acting as a distant regulator of transcription  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 2|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 1|[8]]].

### 3. Metabolic Syndrome (MetS)
*   **Biomarker Identification:** In peripheral blood leukocytes, the *FTO* promoter has been identified as having **differential DNA methylation levels** in patients with both T2DM and metabolic syndrome  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/cheng_2021_comprehensive_analysis_of_dna_methylation_regulator_dna_methyltransferase.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/lijiao_2025_dna_methylation_and_demethylation_in_adipocyte_biology_roles.pdf#Passage 1|[10]]]. Specifically, analytical subgroups of MetS patients showed distinct methylation patterns at the *FTO* promoter compared to controls  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/lijiao_2025_dna_methylation_and_demethylation_in_adipocyte_biology_roles.pdf#Passage 1|[10]]].

### General Enzymatic Context from the Sources
While the specific enzyme for *FTO* is not named, the sources provide the following general context for the enzymes involved in such DNA modifications:
*   **DNMT Family (Writers/Maintainers):** **DNMT3A** and **DNMT3B** typically establish *de novo* methylation patterns, while **DNMT1** maintains them during replication  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 3|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/xiaofeng_2021_methylation_multiplicity_and_its_clinical_values_in_cancer.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/lijiao_2025_dna_methylation_and_demethylation_in_adipocyte_biology_roles.pdf#Passage 2|[11]]].
*   **TET Family (Erasers):** **TET1, TET2, and TET3** catalyze the active demethylation of 5mC through iterative oxidation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 4|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[4]]]. These enzymes require **oxygen, α-ketoglutarate, and iron** to function, and their activity can be enhanced by **Vitamin C** [13, 14]
