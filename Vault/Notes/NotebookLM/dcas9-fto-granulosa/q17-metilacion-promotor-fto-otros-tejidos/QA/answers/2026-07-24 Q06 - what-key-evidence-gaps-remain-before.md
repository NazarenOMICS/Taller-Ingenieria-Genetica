# Q06 - What key evidence gaps remain before proposing FTO promoter methylation as the main cause of FTO downregulation in granulosa aging or oxidative stress?

To propose **FTO promoter methylation** as the primary driver of *FTO* downregulation in **granulosa aging** or **oxidative stress**, several critical evidence gaps must be addressed. While the sources establish that *FTO* expression is regulated by promoter methylation in metabolic contexts, they do not yet provide a direct link to the unique environment of the aging ovary.

The following key evidence gaps remain:

### 1. Lack of Tissue-Specific Data for Granulosa Cells
The sources document that *FTO* protein expression is reduced in **aged human and mouse granulosa cells**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 6|[1]]]. However, the specific evidence linking this downregulation to **DNA methylation** is derived almost entirely from **peripheral blood leukocytes** and **adipose tissue**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 8|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 9|[3]]]. 
*   **The Gap:** DNA methylation is highly **tissue-specific**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 8|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 10|[4]]]. There is currently no direct evidence in the sources confirming that the *FTO* promoter undergoes hypermethylation in granulosa cells during aging or in response to oxidative stress.

### 2. Causal Trigger: Metabolic vs. Aging/Oxidative Stress
The sources provide a clear mechanism for *FTO* methylation changes triggered by **metabolic stimuli** (e.g., high-fat diet, physical inactivity, or Type 2 Diabetes)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 8|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 9|[3]]]. 
*   **The Gap:** There is no reported evidence that **oxidative stress** or **senescence-associated signals** directly induce hypermethylation at the *FTO* promoter. While oxidative stress markers are associated with *FTO* methylation in diabetes cohorts, a direct causal link where stress *causes* the methylation that reduces *FTO* transcription in the ovary has not been established  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 9|[3]]].

### 3. Identification of Functional CpG Loci in the Ovary
Research into *FTO* methylation has identified specific regulatory sites, such as **rs1121980** in the first intron, which acts as a distant regulator of transcription in metabolic diseases  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 9|[3]]].
*   **The Gap:** It is unknown whether the same intronic sites or specific promoter-proximal CpG islands are the relevant regulatory nodes in **granulosa cells**. Because chromatin architecture differs by cell type, the loci governing *FTO* expression in the ovary may be entirely distinct from those identified in blood or fat  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 8|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 10|[4]]].

### 4. Direct Enzymatic Mediation
The sources identify the general roles of **DNMTs** (writers) and **TETs** (erasers) in the DNA methylation cycle  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 8|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 10|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 11|[5]]]. 
*   **The Gap:** No specific enzyme (e.g., DNMT3A or TET2) has been implicated as the direct mediator of the *FTO* promoter’s state in the ovary. Identifying which member of the epigenetic machinery is responsible for silencing *FTO* during aging is a prerequisite for proposing it as a "main cause"  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 3|[6]]].

### 5. The "Chicken or Egg" Paradox
The sources highlight a persistent challenge in epigenetics: determining whether an altered methylation landscape is a **contributing factor** to a disease or a **consequence** of the cellular response to that disease (e.g., to hyperglycemia or age-related inflammation)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 9|[3]]].
*   **The Gap:** In granulosa aging, it is not yet clear if *FTO* promoter hypermethylation is the *initiating* event or a secondary effect of the broader collapse of gene expression networks seen in senescence  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 6|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 9|[3]]].

### 6. Hierarchical Role Among Epigenetic Layers
Transcriptional silencing is rarely driven by DNA methylation alone; it often involves **crosstalk** with histone modifications (e.g., loss of H3K4me3 or gain of H3K27me3) and non-coding RNAs  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 6|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 8|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 10|[4]]].
*   **The Gap:** The sources do not establish whether DNA methylation is the *primary* cause of *FTO* downregulation or if it is subordinate to other epigenetic changes, such as **histone deacetylation**, which also occurs in aging models  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 10|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 12|[7]]].
