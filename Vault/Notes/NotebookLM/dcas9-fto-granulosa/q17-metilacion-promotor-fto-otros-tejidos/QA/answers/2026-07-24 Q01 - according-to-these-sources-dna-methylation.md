# Q01 - According to these sources, is DNA methylation (5mC or 6mA) at the FTO promoter directly reported as a mechanism that reduces FTO transcription in any tissue?

Yes, the sources directly report that **DNA methylation at the *FTO* promoter** is a regulatory mechanism that influences its transcription, particularly in the context of **obesity** and **metabolic disorders**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]].

Specifically, the following findings are detailed regarding **5-methylcytosine (5mC)**:

*   **Silencing through Hypermethylation:** In the context of obesity, **hypermethylation of the *FTO* promoter** is reported to **silence *FTO* expression**, which is associated with a reduced risk of obesity  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]]. 
*   **Activation through Hypomethylation:** Conversely, **hypomethylation of the *FTO* promoter** is reported to **markedly upregulate *FTO* expression**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]]. This upregulation, often induced by factors like a high-fat diet or physical inactivity, modulates the downstream *IRX3/IRX5* gene network, impacting energy expenditure and adipocyte differentiation, thereby increasing individual obesity risk  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]].
*   **Tissue and Markers:** These mechanisms are primarily discussed in relation to **adipose homeostasis** and obesity risk  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/chen_2026_dna_methylation_a_key_epigenetic_regulator_of_cardiovascular.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]]. Additionally, studies utilizing **peripheral blood** have identified differential DNA methylation levels at the *FTO* promoter in patients with **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM)** and metabolic syndrome  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 2|[4]]]. One specific study found a CpG site (rs1121980) in the **first intron** of the *FTO* gene—a region that can function as a distant regulator of transcription—that was significantly **hypomethylated in the blood** of T2DM patients compared to healthy controls  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 3|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/sanabil_2020_the_role_of_dna_methylation_in_the_pathogenesis.pdf#Passage 4|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/lin_2023_epigenetic_targeting_of_autophagy_for_cancer_dna_and.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]].

While the sources extensively discuss **N6-methyladenine (6mA)** as a DNA modification that can regulate transcription by influencing transcription factor accessibility to promoters, they **do not directly report 6mA at the *FTO* promoter** specifically as a mechanism for reducing its transcription  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/lin_2023_epigenetic_targeting_of_autophagy_for_cancer_dna_and.pdf#Passage 2|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/ruochen_2023_methylation_across_the_central_dogma_in_health_and.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/lin_2023_epigenetic_targeting_of_autophagy_for_cancer_dna_and.pdf#Passage 3|[10]]]. Most references to FTO in the context of N6-methylation identify it as an **eraser (demethylase)** of the **m6A mark on RNA**, rather than being a target of DNA 6mA methylation itself  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/ruochen_2023_methylation_across_the_central_dogma_in_health_and.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/ruochen_2023_methylation_across_the_central_dogma_in_health_and.pdf#Passage 2|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/xiao_2026_targeting_epigenetic_regulators_to_boost_t_cell_immunotherapy.pdf#Passage 1|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q17-metilacion-promotor-fto-otros-tejidos/Sources/yan_2026_the_role_of_epigenetic_modifications_in_cancer_associated.pdf#Passage 1|[13]]].
