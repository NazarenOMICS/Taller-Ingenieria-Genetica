# Q03 - Is there evidence that oxidative stress changes C/EBPα occupancy, activity, or promoter binding at FTO or analogous loci in any cell type?

While the sources do not provide direct evidence that oxidative stress specifically changes **C/EBPα occupancy** at the ***FTO* promoter**, they do establish C/EBPα as a key regulator of *FTO* and provide extensive evidence of oxidative stress directing transcription factor occupancy at **analogous stress-responsive loci**.

### **C/EBPα and the *FTO* Promoter**
The sources confirm that C/EBPα is a **positive regulatory factor** that binds directly to a highly conserved site near the transcription start site of the human *FTO* gene to activate its transcription  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/zewei_2024_insights_into_the_m6a_demethylases_fto_and_alkbh5.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]]. While FTO itself is essential for maintaining mitochondrial integrity and limiting **reactive oxygen species (ROS)** generation, its transcriptional activation during certain stress responses (such as ischemia-reperfusion) is specifically linked to the transcription factor **SP1** rather than reported changes in C/EBPα occupancy  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 2|[4]]].

### **Evidence at Analogous Loci**
The sources demonstrate that oxidative stress ($H_2O_2$ treatment) acts as a primary trigger for altering the promoter occupancy of other transcription factors and co-factors at analogous loci:

*   **CTCF at the *SIRT1* Promoter:** Oxidative stress induces the **phosphorylation of CTCF** by the kinase CK2  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/tobias_2024_recruitment_of_ctcf_to_the_sirt1_promoter_after.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/tobias_2024_recruitment_of_ctcf_to_the_sirt1_promoter_after.pdf#Passage 2|[6]]]. This post-translational modification acts as a "rheostat," permitting **CTCF to occupy low-affinity DNA sites** at the *SIRT1* promoter that are not occupied under basal conditions  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/tobias_2024_recruitment_of_ctcf_to_the_sirt1_promoter_after.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/tobias_2024_recruitment_of_ctcf_to_the_sirt1_promoter_after.pdf#Passage 3|[7]]]. This recruitment is a cardioprotective response that activates *SIRT1* transcription  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/tobias_2024_recruitment_of_ctcf_to_the_sirt1_promoter_after.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/tobias_2024_recruitment_of_ctcf_to_the_sirt1_promoter_after.pdf#Passage 4|[8]]].
*   **Cyclin C and the Cdk8 Kinase Module (CKM):** Following peroxide stress, **Cyclin C promoter occupancy** changes dynamically in a locus-specific manner  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/david_2020_cyclin_c_promoter_occupancy_directs_changes_in_stress.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]]. It increases at promoters of genes it activates (such as *Trp53inp1* and *p21*) while decreasing at loci it represses under normal conditions (such as *Jmy*), thereby directing transcriptional reprogramming  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/david_2020_cyclin_c_promoter_occupancy_directs_changes_in_stress.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/david_2020_cyclin_c_promoter_occupancy_directs_changes_in_stress.pdf#Passage 2|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/david_2020_cyclin_c_promoter_occupancy_directs_changes_in_stress.pdf#Passage 3|[11]]].
*   **TEAD1 and NRF2:** TEAD1 is identified as a direct transcriptional regulator of **NRF2**, the master regulator of the antioxidant response  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/rajaganapathi_2025_tead1_is_a_novel_regulator_of_nrf2_and.pdf#Passage 1|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/rajaganapathi_2025_tead1_is_a_novel_regulator_of_nrf2_and.pdf#Passage 2|[13]]]. ChIP-seq data confirms **TEAD1 occupancy** at the promoters of multiple antioxidant genes (including *HMOX1*, *SOD1/2*, and *NQO1*) within open chromatin regions, a binding that is essential for a robust cellular response to oxidative stress  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/rajaganapathi_2025_tead1_is_a_novel_regulator_of_nrf2_and.pdf#Passage 3|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/rajaganapathi_2025_tead1_is_a_novel_regulator_of_nrf2_and.pdf#Passage 4|[15]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/rajaganapathi_2025_tead1_is_a_novel_regulator_of_nrf2_and.pdf#Passage 5|[16]]].

### **Redox-Related Regulation of C/EBP Factors**
Although not detailed for occupancy at the *FTO* locus, other C/EBP family members are regulated by stress-related pathways:
*   **C/EBPβ:** Its transcriptional activity is impaired by **SIRT1**, which deacetylates it in response to metabolic shifts  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/wen_2025_dynamic_rendition_of_adipose_genes_under_epigenetic_regulation.pdf#Passage 4|[17]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/zhu_2026_cell_signaling_and_transcriptional_regulation_of_osteoclast_lineage.pdf#Passage 1|[18]]].
*   **C/EBPα Activation:** It can be activated via **AKT signaling** downstream of the NF-κB pathway, which is often involved in stress and inflammatory responses  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q16-cebpa-promotor-fto/Sources/zhu_2026_cell_signaling_and_transcriptional_regulation_of_osteoclast_lineage.pdf#Passage 2|[19]]].
