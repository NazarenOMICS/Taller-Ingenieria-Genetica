# Q01 - According to these sources, how does FTO expression change in the ovary or granulosa cells with age, and in which direction (increase or decrease)?

According to the sources, FTO expression in the ovary and granulosa cells (GCs) **significantly decreases (is downregulated)** with age  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/xiaoyan_2022_the_role_of_m6a_on_female_reproduction_and.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 2|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 3|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 4|[5]]]. 

This chronological and pathological decline is characterized by the following findings in the provided material:

*   **Granulosa Cell Downregulation:** Research indicates that both mRNA and protein levels of the m6A demethylase FTO are significantly lower in the GCs of human aged ovaries compared to those in younger ovaries with a normal ovarian reserve  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 3|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 4|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/xiaolan_2024_m6a_demethylase_fto_stabilized_exosomal_circbrca1_alleviates_oxidative.pdf#Passage 1|[6]]].
*   **Pathological Aging (POI):** This same direction of change—a **decrease** in FTO expression—is observed in patients with **premature ovarian insufficiency (POI)**, a form of pathological ovarian aging  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/xiaolan_2024_m6a_demethylase_fto_stabilized_exosomal_circbrca1_alleviates_oxidative.pdf#Passage 2|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/xiaoyan_2022_the_role_of_m6a_on_female_reproduction_and.pdf#Passage 2|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 5|[10]]].
*   **Consequence of Decreased FTO:** Because FTO serves as an "eraser" that removes m6A marks, its downregulation results in an **abnormal increase in total m6A modification levels** within the granulosa cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/xiaoyan_2022_the_role_of_m6a_on_female_reproduction_and.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 2|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 3|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 5|[10]]].
*   **Role of Oxidative Stress:** The accumulation of **reactive oxygen species (ROS)** in the aging ovary is identified as a primary driver for the decrease in FTO levels  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 6|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 7|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 8|[13]]].
*   **Downstream Effects (FOS Stabilization):** The reduction in FTO expression leads to increased m6A modification on specific targets like **FOS mRNA**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 2|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 7|[12]]]. This modification stabilizes the FOS transcript and impairs its normal degradation, resulting in elevated FOS protein levels that further promote GC senescence and accelerate the overall ovarian aging process  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 2|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/zhongxin_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 7|[12]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/xiaolan_2024_m6a_demethylase_fto_stabilized_exosomal_circbrca1_alleviates_oxidative.pdf#Passage 3|[14]]].
*   **Impact on Mitochondrial Function:** Low FTO expression is also linked to **mitochondrial dysfunction** in granulosa cells, mimicking the effects of aging-related damage  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q05-fto-m6a-edad/Sources/xiaolan_2024_m6a_demethylase_fto_stabilized_exosomal_circbrca1_alleviates_oxidative.pdf#Passage 4|[15]]].

> Ovarian aging is characterized by the constant decreasing of the number and the quality of follicles (Figure 1). The number of primordial follicles peaked at nearly seven million at gestational week 20 but dropped to one million at birth. At the menopausal age of 50 years old, about 1,000 follicles remained in ovary (Jerome and Robert, 2017; Laisk et al., 2019). Moreover, the more erroneous rate of meiosis during oocyte maturation increased with aging (Greaney et al., 2018). The aneuploidy rate of oocytes was 20% at the age of 35 and up to 60% at the age of 45 (Kuliev et al., 2011; Franasiak et al., 2014), resulting in the increasing incidence of aneuploid embryos, accompanied rising miscarriage, birth defection rate, gestational and obstetric complications (Hassold and Hunt, 2001; Frederiksen et al., 2018; Laisk et al., 2019; Correa-de-Araujo and Yoon, 2021). m6A modification and its regulators were essential for ovarian development and its aberrant manifestation resulted in ovarian aging. Jiang et al. found that downregulated FTO and increased m6A in granulosa cells (GCs) were accompanied by ovarian aging. They also reported that FTO slowed down the degradation of FOS-mRNA to upregulate FOS expression in GCs, eventually resulted in GC-mediated ovarian aging (Jiang et al., 2021). In 2009, FTO mutation was reported to cause severe growth retardation and accelerate senescence in the skin fibroblasts (Boissel et al., 2009). Meanwhile, Min et al. identified inconsistent results of m6A level during the aging process, which may be related to the tissue differences between the ovary and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (Min et al., 2018).
