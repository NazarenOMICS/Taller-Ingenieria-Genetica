## Hilo central del bloque

- Based on the provided sources, there is **no direct evidence** that DNA methylation at the *FTO* promoter (or a promoter-proximal CpG island) explains the decreased *FTO* transcription observed under oxidative stress, aging, or ovarian/granulosa contexts.
- Based on the provided sources, there is **no direct evidence or specific proposal** that reduced H3K27ac or HDAC-mediated deacetylation at the *FTO* promoter is the mechanism for *FTO* downregulation in granulosa cells or oxidative stress models.
- Based on the provided sources, several transcription factors are reported to directly regulate the transcription of the ***FTO*** **gene**, primarily acting as activators whose suppression or activation is linked to oxidative stress and reproductive pathologies. No specific proteins are explicitly identified as "promoter-bound repressors" for *FTO* in these contexts; instead, downregulation is typically described as a result of lost activation or complex feedback loops.
- Based on the provided sources, there are **no reports of promoter-region mutations, cis-regulatory variants, or structural alterations that directly silence or reduce *FTO* transcription** in granulosa cells or other tissues. Instead, the sources focus on a specific common genetic variant and attribute transcriptional changes to stress-induced pathways or hormonal signaling.
- Among the candidate mechanisms provided, **transcription factor-mediated regulation (specifically the loss of SP1-mediated activation)** is the only one with direct support in the sources for explaining the control of ***FTO* transcription**, although this evidence is primarily derived from non-granulosa tissues and applied to stress models by extrapolation  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]].
- While the sources provide extensive phenotypic evidence that **FTO expression (mRNA and protein) is significantly downregulated** in human granulosa cells (GCs) under oxidative stress and aging, they leave a substantial **mechanistic gap** regarding the primary events occurring at the *FTO* gene promoter.
