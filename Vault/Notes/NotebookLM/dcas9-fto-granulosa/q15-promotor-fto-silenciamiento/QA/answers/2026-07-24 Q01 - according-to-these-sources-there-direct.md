# Q01 - According to these sources, is there direct evidence that DNA methylation at the FTO promoter (or promoter-proximal CpG island) explains decreased FTO transcription under oxidative stress, aging, or ovarian aging/granulosa contexts?

Based on the provided sources, there is **no direct evidence** that DNA methylation at the *FTO* promoter (or a promoter-proximal CpG island) explains the decreased *FTO* transcription observed under oxidative stress, aging, or ovarian/granulosa contexts.

While the sources extensively document that *FTO* expression (mRNA and protein) decreases in these specific scenarios, they attribute the silencing to other mechanisms or leave the exact transcriptional regulatory cause unstated. Key details from the sources include:

### 1. Evidence of Decreased *FTO* Expression
The sources confirm that *FTO* transcription and protein levels are significantly reduced in various reproductive and stress contexts:
*   **Ovarian Aging:** *FTO* expression drops in the aged mouse ovary and in the granulosa cells (GCs) of the aged human ovary  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/ruochen_2023_methylation_across_the_central_dogma_in_health_and.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/yamada_2025_interplay_of_oxidative_stress_autophagy_and_rubicon_in.pdf#Passage 1|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]].
*   **Oxidative Stress:** Exposure to reactive oxygen species (ROS), such as hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$), or heavy metals like cadmium, leads to a significant downregulation of *FTO* in granulosa cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 1|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/liu_2023_mechanisms_of_and_potential_medications_for_oxidative_stress.pdf#Passage 1|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/xiaolan_2024_m6a_demethylase_fto_stabilized_exosomal_circbrca1_alleviates_oxidative.pdf#Passage 1|[5]]].
*   **Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI):** *FTO* mRNA and protein levels are significantly lower in patients with POI and in corresponding mouse models  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/xiaolan_2024_m6a_demethylase_fto_stabilized_exosomal_circbrca1_alleviates_oxidative.pdf#Passage 2|[6]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/xinru_2024_the_role_of_epigenetics_in_women_s_reproductive.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 2|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/kuai_2026_unravelling_multilayered_rna_modification_networks_in_female_reproduction.pdf#Passage 1|[9]]].
*   **Chemotherapy:** Iatrogenic injury from cisplatin or cyclophosphamide suppresses *FTO* expression in GCs  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/rongli_2021_overexpression_of_fto_protects_human_granulosa_cells_from.pdf#Passage 1|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/rongli_2021_overexpression_of_fto_protects_human_granulosa_cells_from.pdf#Passage 2|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/li_2026_the_role_of_n_lt_sup_gt_6.pdf#Passage 1|[12]]].

### 2. Alternative Mechanisms for *FTO* Regulation
Instead of DNA methylation, the sources identify other factors that regulate *FTO* transcription:
*   **Transcription Factor Activation:** The transcription factor **SP1** is identified as a positive regulator that activates the transcription of *FTO*  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/tianyi_2020_the_potential_role_of_rna_n6_methyladenosine_in.pdf#Passage 1|[13]]].
*   **Transcriptional Feedback Loops:** In certain cancer contexts, *FTO* transcription can be inhibited by **R-2HG** through a feedback loop involving the suppression of **CEBPA**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/rongli_2021_overexpression_of_fto_protects_human_granulosa_cells_from.pdf#Passage 3|[14]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/yamada_2025_interplay_of_oxidative_stress_autophagy_and_rubicon_in.pdf#Passage 2|[15]]].
*   **Stem Cell Secretions:** Co-culture with menstrual-derived stem cells (MenSCs) can restore *FTO* expression in injured granulosa cells, though the specific secreted factors responsible for this transcriptional recovery remain unknown  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/rongli_2021_overexpression_of_fto_protects_human_granulosa_cells_from.pdf#Passage 2|[11]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/yamada_2025_interplay_of_oxidative_stress_autophagy_and_rubicon_in.pdf#Passage 3|[16]]].

### 3. DNA Methylation in Other Genes
The sources do discuss DNA methylation as a silencing mechanism for *other* genes in similar contexts, which may be the source of the question's premise:
*   **Autophagy Genes:** In aging rats, core autophagy genes such as ***Atg5*** and ***LC3B*** exhibit significantly reduced expression accompanied by **pronounced hypermethylation of their promoter regions**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/lina_2025_effects_of_liraglutide_on_leptin_promoter_methylation_in.pdf#Passage 1|[17]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/desmawati_2018_dna_methylation_of_the_androgen_receptor_gene_promoter.pdf#Passage 1|[18]]].
*   **Leptin:** Liraglutide treatment has been shown to increase **leptin (*LEP*) promoter methylation** in the granulosa cells of obese PCOS patients  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/desmawati_2018_dna_methylation_of_the_androgen_receptor_gene_promoter.pdf#Passage 2|[19]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/desmawati_2018_dna_methylation_of_the_androgen_receptor_gene_promoter.pdf#Passage 3|[20]]].
*   **Androgen Receptor:** The ***AR*** **promoter** is hypomethylated in the granulosa cells of PCOS patients, leading to increased expression  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/li_2025_mechanism_of_crosstalk_between_dna_methylation_and_histone.pdf#Passage 1|[21]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/li_2025_mechanism_of_crosstalk_between_dna_methylation_and_histone.pdf#Passage 2|[22]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/kuai_2026_unravelling_multilayered_rna_modification_networks_in_female_reproduction.pdf#Passage 2|[23]]].
*   **Stress Response Genes:** Under aging and oxidative stress, *DNMT1* promotes methylation of the **Sp1 binding site in the *LEDGF* gene promoter CpG island**, suppressing its expression [24, 25].

In summary, while the sources describe a clear correlation between oxidative stress/aging and reduced *FTO* levels, they do not identify *FTO*'s own promoter DNA methylation as the underlying cause. Instead, they focus on the downstream consequences of low *FTO* (such as increased RNA $m^6A$ levels) rather than the upstream epigenetic silencing of the *FTO* gene itself  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/xinru_2024_the_role_of_epigenetics_in_women_s_reproductive.pdf#Passage 1|[7]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q15-promotor-fto-silenciamiento/Sources/yuqian_2023_premature_ovarian_insufficiency_a_review_on_the_role.pdf#Passage 2|[8]]].
