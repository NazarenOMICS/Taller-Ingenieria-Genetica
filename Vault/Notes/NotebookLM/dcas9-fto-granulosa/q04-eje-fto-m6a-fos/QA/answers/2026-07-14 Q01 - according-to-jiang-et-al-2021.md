# Q01 - According to Jiang et al. (2021) and any related sources, what is the proposed mechanism by which the m6A demethylase FTO regulates FOS expression in human granulosa cells?

According to Jiang et al. (2021) and related reviews, the proposed mechanism by which the m6A demethylase **FTO** regulates **FOS** expression is through an epigenetic pathway known as the **FTO-FOS axis**, which plays a critical role in human **ovarian aging**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 2|[2]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 3|[3]]].

The mechanism involves the following key steps:

*   **Initiation by Oxidative Stress:** The process is often initiated by the accumulation of **reactive oxygen species (ROS)**, which acts as a driving force to **downregulate the expression of FTO** (an m6A "eraser") in human ovarian granulosa cells (GCs)  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 4|[4]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 5|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 6|[6]]].
*   **Increased m6A Methylation on FOS mRNA:** FTO normally functions to bind to **FOS mRNA** and erase m6A modifications  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 7|[7]]]. When FTO levels decrease in aged GCs, there is a subsequent **increase in m6A modification** specifically within the **3′ untranslated region (3′UTR)** of FOS mRNA, particularly at "GGAC" consensus sequences  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 5|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 8|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 9|[9]]].
*   **Enhanced mRNA Stability via IGF2BPs:** This increased m6A modification impairs the normal degradation process of FOS mRNA  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 8|[8]]]. The m6A residues are recognized by **IGF2BPs** (insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding proteins), specifically **IGF2BP2**, which act as "readers" that bind to the modified FOS mRNA and **reinforce its stability**, thereby slowing its decay  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 5|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 10|[10]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 11|[11]]].
*   **Upregulation of FOS Expression:** Due to the increased stability of its mRNA, the expression levels of the **FOS protein** are significantly **upregulated** in the granulosa cells of aged ovaries  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 5|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 8|[8]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/sun_2022_the_role_of_m_sup_6_sup_a.pdf#Passage 1|[12]]].
*   **Acceleration of Granulosa Cell Senescence:** FOS is a powerful transcription factor subunit and an aging-related protein  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 8|[8]]]. Its abnormal upregulation triggers **senescence-associated phenotypes** and secretory phenotypes (SASP) in GCs, ultimately resulting in **GC-mediated ovarian aging**  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 3|[3]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 5|[5]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/sun_2022_the_role_of_m_sup_6_sup_a.pdf#Passage 2|[13]]].

This FTO-FOS mechanism is considered a potential diagnostic and therapeutic target for addressing ovarian aging and related reproductive disorders, as **silencing FOS** has been shown to partially alleviate the aging phenotype in FTO-deficient cells  [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/jiang_2021_the_m6a_mrna_demethylase_fto_in_granulosa_cells.pdf#Passage 1|[1]]] [[Notes/NotebookLM/dcas9-fto-granulosa/q04-eje-fto-m6a-fos/Sources/sun_2022_the_role_of_m_sup_6_sup_a.pdf#Passage 2|[13]]].

> Ovarian aging is characterized by the constant decreasing of the number and the quality of follicles (Figure 1). The number of primordial follicles peaked at nearly seven million at gestational week 20 but dropped to one million at birth. At the menopausal age of 50 years old, about 1,000 follicles remained in ovary (Jerome and Robert, 2017; Laisk et al., 2019). Moreover, the more erroneous rate of meiosis during oocyte maturation increased with aging (Greaney et al., 2018). The aneuploidy rate of oocytes was 20% at the age of 35 and up to 60% at the age of 45 (Kuliev et al., 2011; Franasiak et al., 2014), resulting in the increasing incidence of aneuploid embryos, accompanied rising miscarriage, birth defection rate, gestational and obstetric complications (Hassold and Hunt, 2001; Frederiksen et al., 2018; Laisk et al., 2019; Correa-de-Araujo and Yoon, 2021). m6A modification and its regulators were essential for ovarian development and its aberrant manifestation resulted in ovarian aging. Jiang et al. found that downregulated FTO and increased m6A in granulosa cells (GCs) were accompanied by ovarian aging. They also reported that FTO slowed down the degradation of FOS-mRNA to upregulate FOS expression in GCs, eventually resulted in GC-mediated ovarian aging (Jiang et al., 2021). In 2009, FTO mutation was reported to cause severe growth retardation and accelerate senescence in the skin fibroblasts (Boissel et al., 2009). Meanwhile, Min et al. identified inconsistent results of m6A level during the aging process, which may be related to the tissue differences between the ovary and peripheral blood mononuclear cells (Min et al., 2018).
